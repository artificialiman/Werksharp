JAMB PHYSICS - 200 HIGHEST-PROBABILITY UTME QUESTIONS

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 1: MECHANICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. A body is in equilibrium under three coplanar forces. Which condition must be satisfied?
A. The vector sum of all forces is zero
B. All forces must be equal
C. All forces must be parallel
D. The forces must be perpendicular
Answer: A
Explanation: Equilibrium requires both ΣF = 0 (translational) and Στ = 0 (rotational). Exception: A body can have zero net force but still rotate if torques don't cancel.

2. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 4 s. What is the acceleration?
A. 5 m/s²
B. 80 m/s²
C. 0.2 m/s²
D. 4 m/s²
Answer: A
Explanation: a = (v - u)/t = (20 - 0)/4 = 5 m/s². Exception: This assumes constant acceleration; real engines provide variable force.

3. A ball is thrown horizontally from a cliff. Which statement is true?
A. Horizontal velocity remains constant; vertical velocity increases
B. Both velocities increase
C. Horizontal velocity increases; vertical velocity is constant
D. Both velocities remain constant
Answer: A
Explanation: No horizontal force acts (ignoring air resistance); gravity acts vertically. Exception: In practice, air resistance reduces horizontal velocity over time.

4. The velocity of a body at maximum height in projectile motion is:
A. Horizontal component only (vertical = 0)
B. Zero
C. Vertical component only
D. Equal to initial velocity
Answer: A
Explanation: At maximum height, vy = 0; vx = u·cosθ remains. Exception: If projected vertically, velocity is truly zero at max height.

5. A force of 10 N acts on a body of mass 2 kg. What is the acceleration?
A. 5 m/s²
B. 20 m/s²
C. 0.2 m/s²
D. 12 m/s²
Answer: A
Explanation: F = ma → a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s². Exception: Net force must be used; if friction exists, subtract it first.

6. Newton's third law states that action and reaction forces:
A. Are equal, opposite, and act on different bodies
B. Act on the same body
C. Cancel each other out
D. Always produce equilibrium
Answer: A
Explanation: Forces in a Newton's 3rd law pair act on different objects and never cancel. Exception: Common misconception is that they cancel — they can't because they act on different bodies.

7. The momentum of a body is defined as:
A. Mass × velocity
B. Mass × acceleration
C. Force × time
D. Mass × displacement
Answer: A
Explanation: p = mv. It is a vector quantity. Exception: Impulse (Ft) equals change in momentum, not momentum itself.

8. A body of mass 3 kg moving at 4 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary body of mass 1 kg. Final velocity is:
A. 3 m/s
B. 4 m/s
C. 1 m/s
D. 12 m/s
Answer: A
Explanation: Conservation of momentum: 3×4 = (3+1)v → v = 12/4 = 3 m/s. Exception: This is a perfectly inelastic collision; kinetic energy is NOT conserved.

9. The law of conservation of linear momentum applies when:
A. No external net force acts on the system
B. All collisions are elastic
C. Bodies are at rest
D. Friction is present
Answer: A
Explanation: Internal forces cancel in pairs (Newton 3rd); only external forces change system momentum. Exception: Explosions and collisions both conserve momentum if no external net force exists.

10. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?
A. Speed
B. Velocity
C. Force
D. Displacement
Answer: A
Explanation: Speed has magnitude only; velocity has magnitude AND direction. Exception: Speed is the magnitude of velocity — they are numerically equal for uniform motion.

11. The work done by a force is zero when:
A. Force is perpendicular to displacement
B. Force is very small
C. Body is moving fast
D. Force is constant
Answer: A
Explanation: W = Fd·cosθ; when θ = 90°, cos90° = 0. Exception: A centripetal force does no work on circular motion even though the body moves.

12. Kinetic energy of a body is:
A. ½mv²
B. mv²
C. mgh
D. Fv
Answer: A
Explanation: Derived from work-energy theorem. Exception: Rotational KE = ½Iω²; both forms can exist simultaneously in rolling objects.

13. A 2 kg body falls from a height of 5 m. Its KE just before hitting the ground is (g = 10 m/s²):
A. 100 J
B. 50 J
C. 10 J
D. 200 J
Answer: A
Explanation: KE = mgh = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J (all PE converts to KE). Exception: Air resistance reduces KE; answer assumes no energy losses.

14. Power is defined as:
A. Rate of doing work
B. Work × time
C. Force × distance
D. Energy / mass
Answer: A
Explanation: P = W/t = Fv. Unit is Watt. Exception: Instantaneous power = Fv (useful for variable force problems).

15. A machine has efficiency of 80%. If the work input is 500 J, useful work output is:
A. 400 J
B. 425 J
C. 625 J
D. 100 J
Answer: A
Explanation: Efficiency = (output/input) × 100. Output = 0.80 × 500 = 400 J. Exception: The remaining 100 J is dissipated as heat/sound, not destroyed.

16. Which type of friction acts on a stationary body?
A. Static friction
B. Kinetic friction
C. Rolling friction
D. Viscous friction
Answer: A
Explanation: Static friction prevents motion; it adjusts up to a maximum (limiting friction). Exception: Static friction can be greater than kinetic friction — body is harder to start moving than to keep moving.

17. The coefficient of friction is defined as:
A. Ratio of friction force to normal reaction
B. Ratio of weight to area
C. Force per unit mass
D. Normal force divided by friction force
Answer: A
Explanation: μ = F/N. Dimensionless quantity. Exception: μ > 1 is possible for some surface pairs (e.g., rubber on rubber) — it doesn't mean friction exceeds gravity.

18. Centripetal acceleration is directed:
A. Toward the center of the circle
B. Away from the center
C. Tangentially
D. Perpendicular to the radius and the plane
Answer: A
Explanation: a = v²/r, always pointing inward. Exception: "Centrifugal force" is a pseudo-force in rotating frames — it doesn't exist in an inertial frame.

19. A satellite in circular orbit has:
A. Constant speed but changing velocity
B. Constant velocity
C. Zero acceleration
D. Increasing speed
Answer: A
Explanation: Speed is constant; direction changes → velocity changes → acceleration exists (centripetal). Exception: In elliptical orbits, speed varies (Kepler's 2nd law).

20. Escape velocity from Earth's surface is approximately:
A. 11.2 km/s
B. 7.9 km/s
C. 3 × 10⁸ m/s
D. 9.8 m/s
Answer: A
Explanation: v_esc = √(2gR) = √(2 × 9.8 × 6.4×10⁶) ≈ 11,200 m/s. Exception: Orbital velocity (7.9 km/s) is different — it keeps a satellite in orbit, not escaping Earth.

21. The period of a simple pendulum depends on:
A. Length and acceleration due to gravity only
B. Mass of the bob
C. Amplitude of swing
D. Material of the string
Answer: A
Explanation: T = 2π√(L/g). Mass and amplitude cancel for small angles. Exception: For large amplitudes, period slightly increases (non-linear regime).

22. The pressure at a depth h in a fluid is:
A. P = ρgh (plus atmospheric pressure)
B. P = ρg/h
C. P = mgh
D. P = gh/ρ
Answer: A
Explanation: Absolute pressure = atmospheric + ρgh. Gauge pressure = ρgh. Exception: Pressure depends only on depth, not the shape of the container (Pascal's paradox).

23. Archimedes' principle states that upthrust equals:
A. Weight of fluid displaced
B. Weight of the object
C. Volume of the object
D. Mass of the fluid
Answer: A
Explanation: Upthrust = ρ_fluid × V_submerged × g. Exception: A body floats when upthrust = weight; it sinks when weight > upthrust regardless of size.

24. Bernoulli's principle relates to:
A. Faster fluid flow → lower pressure
B. Faster fluid flow → higher pressure
C. Pressure is constant throughout fluid
D. Pressure increases with temperature
Answer: A
Explanation: Conservation of energy in fluid flow: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. Exception: Applies only to non-viscous, incompressible, streamline flow.

25. Hooke's Law states that extension is proportional to force:
A. Provided elastic limit is not exceeded
B. Always
C. Only for metals
D. Only for springs
Answer: A
Explanation: F = ke, valid within elastic limit. Exception: Beyond elastic limit, material deforms plastically; Hooke's Law no longer applies.

SECTION 2: WAVES AND SOUND
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

26. The speed of a wave is related to frequency and wavelength by:
A. v = fλ
B. v = f/λ
C. v = λ/f
D. v = f + λ
Answer: A
Explanation: Wave equation. Exception: Speed depends on the medium, not frequency; changing frequency changes wavelength, not speed.

27. Sound waves are:
A. Longitudinal mechanical waves
B. Transverse electromagnetic waves
C. Transverse mechanical waves
D. Longitudinal electromagnetic waves
Answer: A
Explanation: Sound requires a medium; particles vibrate parallel to wave direction. Exception: Sound can travel through solids, liquids, and gases, but NOT vacuum.

28. The pitch of sound depends on:
A. Frequency
B. Amplitude
C. Wavelength
D. Speed
Answer: A
Explanation: Higher frequency = higher pitch. Exception: Loudness depends on amplitude (intensity), not pitch/frequency.

29. Resonance occurs when:
A. Driving frequency equals natural frequency of the body
B. Amplitude is maximum regardless of frequency
C. Two waves have the same amplitude
D. The medium is rigid
Answer: A
Explanation: At resonance, energy transfer is most efficient → maximum amplitude. Exception: Resonance can be destructive (Tacoma Narrows Bridge) or useful (MRI, musical instruments).

30. The Doppler effect states that observed frequency is higher when:
A. Source and observer approach each other
B. Source and observer move apart
C. Source is stationary
D. Observer is stationary
Answer: A
Explanation: Approaching → compressions arrive more frequently → higher f. Exception: Doppler effect applies to light too (redshift/blueshift); this is used to measure galactic motion.

31. In a stationary wave, points of zero displacement are called:
A. Nodes
B. Antinodes
C. Crests
D. Troughs
Answer: A
Explanation: Nodes are fixed; antinodes have maximum displacement. Exception: A stationary wave stores energy but does not transfer it (unlike progressive waves).

32. The frequency of the first harmonic (fundamental) in an open pipe of length L is:
A. v/2L
B. v/4L
C. v/L
D. 2v/L
Answer: A
Explanation: Open pipe: antinodes at both ends; L = λ/2 → f = v/2L. Exception: Closed pipe has node at closed end: f = v/4L for fundamental — half the frequency of open pipe.

33. Which property of a wave determines its energy?
A. Amplitude
B. Frequency
C. Wavelength
D. Speed
Answer: A
Explanation: Energy ∝ A² for mechanical waves. Exception: For photons (quantum), E = hf — frequency determines energy; amplitude means intensity (number of photons).

34. Total internal reflection occurs when:
A. Angle of incidence exceeds critical angle in denser medium
B. Light enters a denser medium
C. Angle of incidence equals angle of refraction
D. Light travels in vacuum
Answer: A
Explanation: TIR only occurs in denser-to-less-dense transition above critical angle. Exception: Used in optical fibres, periscopes, and diamond cutting to maximize brilliance.

35. Snell's Law states:
A. n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂
B. sinθ₁/sinθ₂ = v₁/v₂
C. Both A and B are equivalent
D. n₁/n₂ = sinθ₂/sinθ₁
Answer: C
Explanation: Both forms are equivalent since n = c/v. Exception: Snell's Law breaks down at very high intensities (non-linear optics) or for evanescent waves at TIR.

36. A converging lens forms a real image when the object is:
A. Beyond the focal point
B. At the focal point
C. Between lens and focal point
D. At infinity only
Answer: A
Explanation: Object beyond F → real, inverted image. At F → image at infinity. Inside F → virtual image. Exception: At 2F, image is same size as object; beyond 2F, image is diminished.

37. The power of a lens in dioptres is:
A. P = 1/f (f in metres)
B. P = f
C. P = 100/f (f in cm)
D. Both A and C are equivalent
Answer: D
Explanation: P = 1/f_m = 100/f_cm. Exception: Diverging lenses have negative power; combined lenses add powers algebraically.

38. Diffraction is most noticeable when:
A. Wavelength is comparable to slit/gap size
B. Wavelength is much smaller than gap
C. Frequency is very high
D. The wave is transverse
Answer: A
Explanation: When λ ≈ gap width, spreading is maximum. Exception: Light diffracts but isn't obvious (very small λ); sound diffracts around buildings (large λ relative to openings).

39. Interference of light demonstrates its:
A. Wave nature
B. Particle nature
C. Electromagnetic nature
D. Transverse nature
Answer: A
Explanation: Constructive and destructive interference are wave properties. Exception: Photoelectric effect demonstrates particle (photon) nature — neither alone explains all light phenomena.

40. In Young's double slit experiment, fringe spacing increases when:
A. Wavelength increases or slit separation decreases
B. Wavelength decreases
C. Slit separation increases
D. Screen moves closer
Answer: A
Explanation: y = λD/d; y increases with λ or D, decreases with d. Exception: The experiment only works with coherent sources; ordinary light bulbs won't produce stable fringes.

SECTION 3: HEAT AND THERMODYNAMICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

41. The relationship between Celsius and Kelvin is:
A. K = °C + 273
B. K = °C - 273
C. K = °C × 273
D. K = °C / 273
Answer: A
Explanation: Absolute zero = -273°C = 0 K. Exception: Some texts use 273.15; JAMB typically accepts 273.

42. The zeroth law of thermodynamics states:
A. If A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in equilibrium with C
B. Energy cannot be created or destroyed
C. Entropy always increases
D. Absolute zero cannot be reached
Answer: A
Explanation: This defines temperature as a measurable property. Exception: This law was formulated after the first and second but is more fundamental — hence "zeroth."

43. The first law of thermodynamics is:
A. ΔU = Q - W (internal energy = heat added minus work done by system)
B. Heat flows from cold to hot
C. Entropy always increases
D. Energy is proportional to temperature
Answer: A
Explanation: Conservation of energy for thermodynamic systems. Exception: Sign convention varies — some texts write ΔU = Q + W (work done ON system); read context carefully.

44. Specific heat capacity is defined as:
A. Heat required to raise 1 kg of substance by 1 K
B. Heat to melt 1 kg at its melting point
C. Heat per unit volume
D. Energy per molecule
Answer: A
Explanation: Q = mcΔT. Exception: Water has unusually high specific heat (4200 J/kg/K) — moderates climate; metals have low values (easier to heat).

45. The specific latent heat of vaporization is greater than latent heat of fusion because:
A. More energy needed to separate molecules completely (gas) than partially (liquid)
B. Boiling requires higher temperature
C. Gases are lighter
D. Fusion occurs faster
Answer: A
Explanation: Vaporization breaks nearly all intermolecular bonds; fusion only weakens them. Exception: This is why sweating cools body so effectively — latent heat of vaporization of water is very high.

46. Boyle's Law states (at constant temperature):
A. PV = constant
B. P/V = constant
C. P + V = constant
D. P = V
Answer: A
Explanation: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. Exception: Boyle's Law applies only to ideal gases; real gases deviate at high pressures.

47. Charles' Law states (at constant pressure):
A. V/T = constant (T in Kelvin)
B. V × T = constant
C. V = T (any unit)
D. V/T = constant (T in Celsius)
Answer: A
Explanation: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂; T must be in Kelvin. Exception: Using Celsius gives wrong answers; always convert to Kelvin.

48. The ideal gas equation is:
A. PV = nRT
B. PV = RT
C. PV = NRT
D. P = nRT/V²
Answer: A
Explanation: n = moles, R = 8.314 J/mol/K. Exception: Real gases use van der Waals equation; ideal gas assumes no intermolecular forces and zero molecular volume.

49. Heat transfer by conduction requires:
A. Physical contact or medium with temperature gradient
B. Fluid motion
C. Electromagnetic waves
D. Vacuum
Answer: A
Explanation: Conduction transfers energy through particle vibration. Exception: Metals are good conductors (free electrons carry energy); non-metals and gases are poor conductors.

50. Radiation is the only mode of heat transfer that:
A. Does not require a medium
B. Requires fluid motion
C. Requires physical contact
D. Requires a temperature gradient only
Answer: A
Explanation: Radiation (electromagnetic waves) travels through vacuum. Exception: The Sun heats Earth by radiation across 150 million km of near-vacuum.

51. A good absorber of radiation is also:
A. A good emitter (black body principle)
B. A good reflector
C. A poor emitter
D. Transparent
Answer: A
Explanation: Kirchhoff's Law: emissivity = absorptivity at thermal equilibrium. Exception: A perfect black body absorbs and emits all radiation; polished silver is a poor absorber and poor emitter.

52. Stefan's Law states that power radiated is proportional to:
A. T⁴ (fourth power of absolute temperature)
B. T
C. T²
D. T³
Answer: A
Explanation: P = σAT⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann law). Exception: Small temperature changes cause large changes in radiation — doubling T increases radiated power 16-fold.

53. The greenhouse effect involves:
A. Atmosphere absorbing outgoing infrared, trapping heat
B. Plants absorbing sunlight directly
C. Reflection of sunlight back to Earth
D. Conduction through the ground
Answer: A
Explanation: Short-wave solar radiation enters; re-emitted long-wave IR is absorbed by CO₂, H₂O. Exception: The natural greenhouse effect is essential for life; enhanced greenhouse effect due to human emissions is the concern.

54. Linear expansivity (α) relates to:
A. Change in length per unit original length per degree temperature change
B. Change in volume per degree
C. Change in density per degree
D. Change in mass per degree
Answer: A
Explanation: ΔL = L₀αΔT. Exception: Cubic expansivity γ ≈ 3α; area expansivity β ≈ 2α for isotropic materials.

55. Water has maximum density at:
A. 4°C
B. 0°C
C. 100°C
D. -4°C
Answer: A
Explanation: Anomalous expansion: water expands below 4°C due to hydrogen bond restructuring into ice lattice. Exception: This allows aquatic life to survive winter — ice forms on surface, water at 4°C sinks.

SECTION 4: ELECTRICITY AND MAGNETISM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

56. Ohm's Law states:
A. V = IR (at constant temperature)
B. V = I/R
C. V = I + R
D. R = VI
Answer: A
Explanation: Current is directly proportional to potential difference when temperature is constant. Exception: Ohm's Law applies to ohmic conductors; diodes, thermistors, and LDRs are non-ohmic.

57. The resistance of a conductor depends on:
A. Length, cross-sectional area, and resistivity
B. Voltage applied
C. Current flowing through it
D. Color of conductor
Answer: A
Explanation: R = ρL/A. Exception: Resistance also depends on temperature; metals increase, semiconductors decrease with temperature.

58. Resistors in series: total resistance is:
A. Sum of individual resistances (R_T = R₁ + R₂ + ...)
B. Reciprocal of sum of reciprocals
C. Product of resistances
D. Average of resistances
Answer: A
Explanation: Same current flows through all; voltages add. Exception: Current is the same in series; voltage divides proportionally.

59. Resistors in parallel: total resistance is:
A. 1/R_T = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...
B. R₁ + R₂ + ...
C. (R₁ × R₂)/(R₁ + R₂) for two only
D. Both A and C (C is derived from A for two resistors)
Answer: D
Explanation: For two parallel resistors: R_T = R₁R₂/(R₁ + R₂). Exception: Parallel resistance is always less than the smallest individual resistor.

60. Electric power dissipated in a resistor is:
A. P = VI = I²R = V²/R
B. P = V + I
C. P = IR
D. P = V/I
Answer: A
Explanation: All three forms are equivalent via Ohm's Law. Exception: Power is dissipated as heat; this is useful in heaters but wasteful in transmission lines.

61. Kirchhoff's current law states:
A. Sum of currents entering a junction = sum leaving (charge conservation)
B. Sum of voltages in a loop = zero
C. Current is the same everywhere in a circuit
D. Resistance adds in series
Answer: A
Explanation: KCL is based on conservation of charge. Exception: KCL applies to nodes (junctions); KVL (Kirchhoff's voltage law) applies to loops.

62. Kirchhoff's voltage law states:
A. Sum of EMFs = sum of voltage drops in any closed loop
B. Voltage is constant throughout a circuit
C. Sum of currents = sum of voltages
D. Resistance equals voltage
Answer: A
Explanation: KVL: ΣV = 0 around any closed loop (energy conservation). Exception: Must choose a consistent direction (clockwise or counterclockwise) and apply sign conventions carefully.

63. The EMF of a cell is defined as:
A. Total energy supplied per unit charge (includes internal resistance effects)
B. Terminal voltage when current flows
C. Current × external resistance only
D. Voltage between terminals always
Answer: A
Explanation: EMF = terminal voltage + voltage drop across internal resistance: ε = V + Ir. Exception: EMF equals terminal voltage only when no current flows (open circuit).

64. The terminal voltage of a battery decreases when:
A. Current drawn increases (due to internal resistance drop)
B. Temperature increases
C. Battery is new
D. External resistance increases
Answer: A
Explanation: V = ε - Ir; as I increases, Ir increases, V decreases. Exception: This is why car headlights dim when starter motor is engaged — large current causes large Ir drop.

65. Capacitance is defined as:
A. Charge stored per unit potential difference (C = Q/V)
B. Energy stored per unit charge
C. Charge per unit area
D. Potential per unit charge
Answer: A
Explanation: Unit is Farad (F). Exception: Farad is very large; practical capacitors are measured in μF, nF, or pF.

66. Energy stored in a capacitor is:
A. W = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C
B. W = CV
C. W = QV
D. W = Q²C
Answer: A
Explanation: All three forms are equivalent. Exception: Unlike a battery (energy = QV), capacitor energy is ½QV — the ½ comes from average voltage during charging.

67. Capacitors in parallel: total capacitance is:
A. Sum of individual capacitances
B. Reciprocal of sum of reciprocals
C. Product of capacitances
D. Difference of capacitances
Answer: A
Explanation: Same voltage across all; charges add. Exception: Note this is opposite to resistors in series — capacitors add in parallel, resistors add in series.

68. Magnetic flux density B at center of a long solenoid is:
A. B = μ₀nI (n = turns per unit length)
B. B = μ₀NI (N = total turns)
C. B = μ₀I/L
D. B = μ₀I/(2πr)
Answer: A
Explanation: n = N/L. Exception: B = μ₀I/(2πr) is for a long straight wire, not solenoid.

69. Lenz's Law states:
A. Induced current opposes the change causing it
B. Induced EMF equals rate of change of flux
C. Current flows in direction of magnetic field
D. Increasing flux induces zero EMF
Answer: A
Explanation: Lenz's Law is a consequence of energy conservation. Exception: It is the qualitative version of Faraday's Law: ε = -dΦ/dt (the negative sign embodies Lenz's Law).

70. Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction:
A. EMF induced = rate of change of magnetic flux linkage
B. EMF = BIL
C. EMF = current × resistance
D. Flux = EMF × time
Answer: A
Explanation: ε = -NdΦ/dt. Exception: The magnitude of EMF depends on HOW FAST flux changes, not just the magnitude of flux.

71. Fleming's right-hand rule is used for:
A. Generators (direction of induced current)
B. Motors (direction of force on conductor)
C. Direction of magnetic field from current
D. Direction of current in resistors
Answer: A
Explanation: Right hand: generator (dynamo). Left hand: motor (force on current). Exception: This is a common exam trick — generator = right, motor = left.

72. A transformer works on the principle of:
A. Mutual electromagnetic induction
B. Ohm's Law
C. Self-induction only
D. Electrostatic induction
Answer: A
Explanation: Changing current in primary → changing flux → EMF in secondary. Exception: Transformers only work on AC; DC produces no changing flux and thus no induced EMF.

73. For a step-up transformer:
A. N_s > N_p, V_s > V_p, I_s < I_p
B. N_s < N_p, V_s > V_p
C. V_s > V_p and I_s > I_p simultaneously
D. Power output exceeds power input
Answer: A
Explanation: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p; power is conserved (V_sI_s = V_pI_p ideally). Exception: Energy is conserved; a step-up in voltage means step-down in current.

74. The force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field is:
A. F = BIL (when field and current are perpendicular)
B. F = BIL cosθ
C. F = BI/L
D. F = B/IL
Answer: A
Explanation: F = BILsinθ; maximum when θ = 90°. Exception: No force when current is parallel to field (sinθ = 0).

75. The Hall effect demonstrates:
A. Charge carriers in conductors can be deflected by magnetic fields
B. Magnetic fields are caused by moving charges
C. Electric fields deflect magnetic field lines
D. Resistance increases with magnetic field
Answer: A
Explanation: Produces Hall voltage perpendicular to both current and field. Exception: The sign of Hall voltage reveals whether carriers are positive or negative — key evidence for positive holes in semiconductors.

SECTION 5: OPTICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

76. The law of reflection states:
A. Angle of incidence = angle of reflection (measured from normal)
B. Angle of incidence = angle of refraction
C. Reflected ray is perpendicular to incident ray
D. Reflection occurs only at smooth surfaces
Answer: A
Explanation: Angles measured from the normal, not the surface. Exception: Diffuse reflection from rough surfaces still obeys the law at each microscopic point.

77. A concave mirror forms a virtual image when the object is:
A. Between the focus and the pole
B. Beyond the center of curvature
C. At the center of curvature
D. At the focus
Answer: A
Explanation: Object inside F → virtual, erect, magnified image (like a makeup mirror). Exception: All other positions give real, inverted images of varying sizes.

78. The mirror formula is:
A. 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
B. 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
C. f = u + v
D. f = uv
Answer: A
Explanation: Sign convention must be consistent. Exception: For convex mirrors, f is positive if using "real is positive" or negative in the "new Cartesian" convention — read the question carefully.

79. Magnification of a mirror is:
A. m = -v/u = image height / object height
B. m = v + u
C. m = f/u
D. m = u/v
Answer: A
Explanation: Negative m means inverted image; |m| > 1 means magnified. Exception: The negative sign is often omitted when only size (not orientation) is asked.

80. Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in cars?
A. Convex mirror (gives wider field of view)
B. Concave mirror
C. Plane mirror
D. Parabolic mirror
Answer: A
Explanation: Convex mirror diverges light; always produces virtual, erect, diminished image with larger field of view. Exception: Concave mirrors are used in headlights and search lights (converging function).

81. The refractive index of glass relative to air is 1.5. The critical angle is:
A. sin⁻¹(1/1.5) ≈ 41.8°
B. sin⁻¹(1.5) (impossible)
C. 45°
D. 60°
Answer: A
Explanation: sinC = 1/n = 1/1.5 = 0.667 → C ≈ 41.8°. Exception: Critical angle only exists when going from denser to rarer medium; glass to air, not air to glass.

82. A person with short-sightedness (myopia) uses:
A. Concave (diverging) lens
B. Convex (converging) lens
C. Cylindrical lens
D. Plane glass
Answer: A
Explanation: Myopia: eyeball too long; image forms in front of retina. Concave lens diverges light to move image back to retina. Exception: Long-sight (hyperopia) uses convex lens; both are called spectacle corrections.

83. The human eye focuses objects at different distances by:
A. Changing lens curvature (accommodation)
B. Changing pupil size
C. Moving the retina
D. Moving the lens backward/forward
Answer: A
Explanation: Ciliary muscles adjust lens thickness. Exception: Cameras focus by moving the lens; eyes focus by changing lens shape — fundamentally different mechanism.

84. Optical fibres use total internal reflection to:
A. Transmit light signals along the fibre
B. Amplify light signals
C. Change light frequency
D. Reduce refraction losses
Answer: A
Explanation: Light reflects off fibre walls without escaping as long as angle exceeds critical angle. Exception: Signal can degrade due to dispersion (different wavelengths travel at different speeds) over long distances.

85. Dispersion of white light through a prism occurs because:
A. Different wavelengths have different refractive indices
B. Red light travels faster than blue light in all media
C. The prism absorbs some colors
D. Wavelength changes at the prism surface
Answer: A
Explanation: Shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) refract more → separate from longer wavelengths. Exception: Dispersion produces a spectrum (ROYGBIV); red deviates least, violet most.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 6: ATOMIC AND NUCLEAR PHYSICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

86. The photoelectric effect shows that light consists of:
A. Photons (particles with energy E = hf)
B. Waves only
C. Electrons
D. Protons
Answer: A
Explanation: Einstein explained that photons must have enough energy (hf ≥ work function) to eject electrons. Exception: Below threshold frequency, no electrons are emitted regardless of intensity — proof of quantization.

87. The work function of a metal is:
A. Minimum energy needed to eject an electron from the surface
B. Total energy of all electrons
C. Energy of incident photon
D. Kinetic energy of emitted electron
Answer: A
Explanation: KE_max = hf - φ (Einstein's photoelectric equation). Exception: If hf = φ, electrons are just emitted with zero kinetic energy.

88. In the Rutherford model of the atom:
A. Nucleus contains protons; electrons orbit at large distances
B. Electrons are embedded in a positive sphere
C. Nucleus contains both protons and electrons
D. Atom has no nucleus
Answer: A
Explanation: Rutherford gold foil experiment showed most of atom is empty space; nucleus is tiny and dense. Exception: Rutherford's model couldn't explain atomic spectra or electron stability — Bohr improved it.

89. The nucleus of an atom contains:
A. Protons and neutrons only
B. Protons, neutrons, and electrons
C. Protons and electrons
D. Neutrons only
Answer: A
Explanation: Electrons orbit outside; nucleus = protons + neutrons (nucleons). Exception: Hydrogen has no neutrons in its most common isotope (¹H); it is unique.

90. Alpha particles are:
A. Helium-4 nuclei (2 protons, 2 neutrons, charge +2)
B. High-energy electrons
C. Electromagnetic radiation
D. Single protons
Answer: A
Explanation: α = ⁴₂He²⁺. Exception: Alpha particles are most ionizing but least penetrating (stopped by paper or a few cm of air).

91. Beta particles (β⁻) are:
A. High-speed electrons emitted from the nucleus
B. Helium nuclei
C. X-rays
D. Neutrons
Answer: A
Explanation: A neutron converts to proton + electron + antineutrino. Exception: β⁺ (positron emission) also exists — a proton converts to a neutron + positron + neutrino.

92. Gamma rays are:
A. High-energy electromagnetic radiation from the nucleus
B. High-speed electrons
C. Helium nuclei
D. Stream of neutrons
Answer: A
Explanation: γ rays have no charge, no mass; most penetrating (need lead or thick concrete to stop). Exception: Gamma emission accompanies alpha or beta decay — it is the nucleus releasing excess energy.

93. The half-life of a radioactive substance is:
A. Time for half the nuclei to decay
B. Half the total decay time
C. Time for all nuclei to decay
D. Time for activity to double
Answer: A
Explanation: T₁/₂ = ln2/λ = 0.693/λ. Exception: After n half-lives, fraction remaining = (½)ⁿ. After 10 half-lives, < 0.1% remains.

94. Nuclear fission involves:
A. Heavy nucleus splitting into two lighter nuclei + energy
B. Two light nuclei combining
C. Nucleus emitting an alpha particle only
D. Proton absorbing a neutron
Answer: A
Explanation: Fission releases energy because binding energy per nucleon increases (products are more stable). Exception: Fission of ²³⁵U releases ~200 MeV; chemical reactions release ~10 eV — nuclear is ~10⁷ times more energetic.

95. Nuclear fusion involves:
A. Light nuclei combining to form heavier nucleus + energy
B. Heavy nucleus splitting
C. Electron capture by nucleus
D. Neutron decay
Answer: A
Explanation: Source of energy in the Sun (H → He). Exception: Fusion requires enormous temperatures (~10⁷ K) to overcome Coulomb repulsion; not yet commercially viable on Earth.

96. Mass-energy equivalence is given by:
A. E = mc²
B. E = mv²
C. E = hf only
D. E = Fc
Answer: A
Explanation: Einstein's equation; c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Exception: E = mc² applies to rest mass energy; total energy includes kinetic energy for moving particles.

97. Isotopes of an element have:
A. Same atomic number (Z), different mass number (A)
B. Same mass number, different atomic number
C. Same number of neutrons
D. Same physical properties
Answer: A
Explanation: Same Z means same chemical properties (same electron arrangement). Exception: Isotopes have different physical properties (mass, density) and some are radioactive while others are stable.

98. The atomic number (Z) of an element equals:
A. Number of protons (= number of electrons in neutral atom)
B. Number of neutrons
C. Mass number
D. Number of nucleons
Answer: A
Explanation: Z identifies the element. Exception: In ions, electron count ≠ proton count; Z still equals proton count.

99. X-rays are produced when:
A. Fast electrons decelerate rapidly hitting a metal target (Bremsstrahlung)
B. Nuclei undergo radioactive decay
C. Alpha particles are emitted
D. Neutrons bombard atoms
Answer: A
Explanation: Also produced by electron transitions in inner shells. Exception: X-rays and gamma rays are both electromagnetic radiation; they differ in origin (X-rays from electrons, gamma from nucleus).

100. The Geiger-Müller (GM) tube detects radiation by:
A. Ionization of gas causing electrical pulses
B. Fluorescence of a screen
C. Photographic plate exposure
D. Heating of a thermocouple
Answer: A
Explanation: Each ionizing particle creates an avalanche of ions → pulse of current → detected and counted. Exception: GM tubes detect but cannot distinguish between α, β, and γ without additional shielding experiments.

SECTION 7: ENERGY, WORK, AND SIMPLE MACHINES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

101. The mechanical advantage (MA) of a machine is:
A. Load / Effort
B. Effort / Load
C. Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load
D. Work output / Work input
Answer: A
Explanation: MA > 1 means machine multiplies force. Exception: For an ideal machine MA × VR = 100% efficiency. In practice, MA < VR due to friction.

102. The velocity ratio (VR) of a pulley system with n pulleys is:
A. VR = n (number of rope segments supporting load)
B. VR = 1 always
C. VR = Load/Effort
D. VR = n²
Answer: A
Explanation: VR is determined by geometry. Exception: A single fixed pulley has VR = 1 (no mechanical advantage for force, only direction change).

103. Efficiency of a machine is:
A. (MA/VR) × 100% = (Work output/Work input) × 100%
B. Load × Distance
C. Effort × VR
D. VR/MA
Answer: A
Explanation: Always less than 100% due to friction and energy losses. Exception: Efficiency can never equal 100% for real machines (contradicts thermodynamics).

104. The principle of conservation of energy states:
A. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another
B. All energy converts to kinetic energy
C. Total energy decreases over time
D. Potential energy is always conserved
Answer: A
Explanation: Total energy of an isolated system is constant. Exception: Mass-energy equivalence extends this to include mass as a form of energy (E = mc²).

105. Which of the following is NOT a renewable energy source?
A. Coal
B. Solar
C. Wind
D. Hydroelectric
Answer: A
Explanation: Coal is fossil fuel (non-renewable). Exception: Nuclear energy produces no CO₂ but fuel (uranium) is finite — categorized as low-carbon but non-renewable.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 8: MOTION AND CIRCULAR MOTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

106. Angular velocity (ω) is related to linear velocity (v) by:
A. v = ωr
B. ω = vr
C. v = ω/r
D. r = ωv
Answer: A
Explanation: At radius r from center, v = ωr. Exception: All points on a rigid body have the same ω but different v (outer points move faster).

107. Centripetal force is:
A. The net inward force needed to maintain circular motion
B. A force pushing outward
C. An additional force separate from friction/tension
D. Always equal to gravity
Answer: A
Explanation: F_c = mv²/r = mω²r. It is provided by friction, tension, gravity, etc. Exception: "Centrifugal force" is not a real force — it's a fictitious force felt in rotating frames.

108. A body moving in uniform circular motion has:
A. Constant speed but continually changing direction (hence accelerating)
B. Constant velocity
C. Zero acceleration
D. Constant kinetic energy (since |v| is constant) — BOTH A AND D are true
Answer: D
Explanation: Speed (and thus KE) is constant; direction (and thus velocity) changes; centripetal acceleration exists. Exception: Acceleration is centripetal — perpendicular to velocity, so it changes direction but not speed.

109. The period of circular motion is related to angular velocity by:
A. T = 2π/ω
B. T = ω/2π
C. T = 2πω
D. T = 1/ω
Answer: A
Explanation: ω = 2π/T (one full revolution = 2π radians). Exception: Frequency f = 1/T; ω = 2πf.

110. Kepler's third law states:
A. T² ∝ R³ (square of period proportional to cube of orbital radius)
B. Planets sweep equal areas in equal times
C. Orbits are ellipses with Sun at one focus
D. T ∝ R
Answer: A
Explanation: T²/R³ = constant for all planets around same star. Exception: Kepler's 2nd law (equal areas in equal times) reflects conservation of angular momentum.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 9: ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

111. The electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency is:
A. Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → UV → X-ray → Gamma
B. Gamma → X-ray → UV → Visible → IR → Microwave → Radio
C. Visible → UV → X-ray → Gamma → Radio → Microwave → IR
D. IR → Visible → UV → Radio → Microwave → X-ray → Gamma
Answer: A
Explanation: Frequency increases; wavelength decreases in the same order. Exception: All EM waves travel at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum, regardless of frequency.

112. Radio waves are used in:
A. Broadcasting and telecommunications
B. Medical imaging (bones)
C. Sterilizing medical equipment
D. TV remote controls
Answer: A
Explanation: Long wavelength → penetrates atmosphere easily. Exception: Microwaves (a subset close to radio) are used in radar and cooking; assignment varies with subregion.

113. Microwaves cause heating because:
A. They cause water molecules to rotate, generating heat from friction
B. They ionize atoms
C. They have high frequency
D. They convert to infrared on absorption
Answer: A
Explanation: Resonant absorption by water molecules at 2.45 GHz (microwave ovens). Exception: Microwaves do NOT cook from the inside out — they penetrate a few cm into food.

114. Infrared radiation is associated with:
A. Heat emission from warm bodies
B. Ionization of gases
C. Nuclear reactions
D. Fluorescence
Answer: A
Explanation: All objects above absolute zero emit IR; warmer = more IR. Exception: Night-vision cameras detect IR — they "see" heat, not light.

115. UV radiation is responsible for:
A. Causing sunburn and vitamin D synthesis in skin
B. Heating the atmosphere
C. Radar detection
D. Medical X-rays
Answer: A
Explanation: UV from Sun ionizes skin molecules; controlled exposure produces vitamin D. Exception: Ozone layer absorbs most UV-B and UV-C; UV-A largely reaches Earth's surface.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 10: MEASUREMENT AND UNITS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

116. The SI unit of force is:
A. Newton (N = kg·m/s²)
B. Joule
C. Watt
D. Pascal
Answer: A
Explanation: F = ma; 1 N = 1 kg × 1 m/s². Exception: Weight is a force (measured in N), not mass (measured in kg) — common source of confusion.

117. The SI unit of pressure is:
A. Pascal (Pa = N/m²)
B. Newton
C. Joule
D. Bar
Answer: A
Explanation: P = F/A; 1 Pa = 1 N/m². Exception: Atmospheric pressure ≈ 10⁵ Pa = 1 bar = 76 cmHg = 1 atm; multiple units in common use.

118. Dimensional analysis of kinetic energy (½mv²) gives:
A. ML²T⁻² (same as work and all energies)
B. MLT⁻¹
C. ML²T⁻¹
D. MLT⁻²
Answer: A
Explanation: [½mv²] = kg·(m/s)² = kg·m²/s² = ML²T⁻². Exception: All energy forms have same dimensions; Joule is the SI unit for all of them.

119. The vernier caliper can measure to:
A. 0.1 mm or 0.01 mm depending on the vernier scale
B. 0.001 mm
C. 1 mm only
D. 10 mm
Answer: A
Explanation: Common vernier: 0.1 mm (10-division) or 0.02 mm (50-division). Exception: Micrometer screw gauge reads to 0.01 mm; for precision below that, optical/laser methods are used.

120. Random errors can be reduced by:
A. Taking multiple readings and averaging
B. Calibrating the instrument
C. Using a different instrument
D. Waiting for stable conditions
Answer: A
Explanation: Random errors vary unpredictably; averaging reduces their effect. Exception: Systematic errors (zero error, calibration faults) are NOT reduced by averaging — they must be identified and corrected.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 11: ELECTROSTATICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

121. Coulomb's Law states:
A. F = kQ₁Q₂/r² (force between two point charges)
B. F = kQ₁Q₂r²
C. F = Q₁Q₂/4πr
D. F = εQ₁Q₂/r
Answer: A
Explanation: k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C². Exception: Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Force is along the line joining the charges.

122. Electric field strength (E) is defined as:
A. Force per unit positive test charge (E = F/q)
B. Force times charge
C. Charge per unit area
D. Potential times distance
Answer: A
Explanation: Unit: N/C = V/m. Exception: The test charge must be small enough not to disturb the field being measured.

123. Electric potential at a distance r from a point charge Q is:
A. V = kQ/r
B. V = kQ/r²
C. V = kQr
D. V = Q/4πr²
Answer: A
Explanation: V is a scalar; potential energy U = qV. Exception: Field E ∝ 1/r² but potential V ∝ 1/r — important distinction for JAMB.

124. The electric field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is:
A. Zero
B. Maximum
C. Equal to surface field
D. Proportional to charge
Answer: A
Explanation: Free electrons redistribute until no net force acts — any field would cause current flow. Exception: Faraday cage shields interior from external electric fields — used in electronics and microwave ovens.

125. Charge resides on the outer surface of a conductor because:
A. Charges repel and move as far apart as possible
B. Conductors are heavy
C. Interior resists charge
D. Charges are attracted to surface
Answer: A
Explanation: Mutual repulsion pushes charges to the surface. Exception: On a non-spherical conductor, charge density is highest at sharp points (used in lightning rods — points leak charge to air).

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 12: DIRECT AND ALTERNATING CURRENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

126. The RMS (root mean square) value of AC voltage is:
A. V_rms = V₀/√2 ≈ 0.707 V₀
B. V_rms = V₀/2
C. V_rms = V₀
D. V_rms = 2V₀
Answer: A
Explanation: RMS is the DC equivalent that would produce same power. Exception: Nigerian mains supply is 240 V RMS; peak voltage is 240√2 ≈ 340 V.

127. In a purely resistive AC circuit, voltage and current are:
A. In phase with each other
B. 90° out of phase
C. 180° out of phase
D. 45° out of phase
Answer: A
Explanation: No energy storage in resistors; instantaneous power = V·I always positive. Exception: Capacitors cause current to lead voltage by 90°; inductors cause current to lag by 90°.

128. In a purely capacitive AC circuit:
A. Current leads voltage by 90°
B. Current lags voltage by 90°
C. Current and voltage are in phase
D. No current flows
Answer: A
Explanation: Capacitor charges/discharges; current is maximum when voltage is changing fastest (at zero crossing). Exception: The phrase is remembered as "CIVIL" — In a Capacitor, I leads V; In a (L) inductor, V leads I.

129. Inductive reactance (X_L) is:
A. X_L = ωL = 2πfL (increases with frequency)
B. X_L = 1/ωL
C. X_L = ωC
D. X_L = L/ω
Answer: A
Explanation: Inductive reactance opposes changing current; more opposition at higher frequency. Exception: A DC signal (f = 0) passes through an inductor with zero reactance — only DC resistance.

130. Capacitive reactance (X_C) is:
A. X_C = 1/ωC = 1/(2πfC) (decreases with frequency)
B. X_C = ωC
C. X_C = ωL
D. X_C = 1/ωL
Answer: A
Explanation: Capacitor blocks DC (X_C → ∞ at f = 0) but passes AC more easily at higher frequencies. Exception: Opposite behavior to inductors — key for filter design.

131. At resonance in an LCR series circuit:
A. X_L = X_C, impedance is minimum (= R), current is maximum
B. Impedance is maximum
C. Voltage across L equals voltage across C only
D. Frequency equals 1 Hz
Answer: A
Explanation: f₀ = 1/(2π√LC). Exception: At resonance, voltages across L and C can greatly exceed supply voltage — called voltage magnification.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 13: PROPERTIES OF MATTER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

132. Young's Modulus (E) is defined as:
A. Stress / Strain
B. Strain / Stress
C. Force × Extension
D. Load / Area
Answer: A
Explanation: E = (F/A)/(ΔL/L₀) = FL₀/AΔL. Unit: Pa (N/m²). Exception: Higher Young's modulus = stiffer material. Steel (~200 GPa) is stiffer than rubber (~0.01 GPa).

133. Surface tension causes:
A. Liquid surfaces to behave like stretched elastic membranes
B. Liquids to expand when heated
C. Pressure to increase with depth
D. Viscosity to decrease
Answer: A
Explanation: Surface tension results from net inward forces on surface molecules. Exception: Water striders walk on water using surface tension; detergent breaks it by reducing intermolecular forces.

134. Capillarity (capillary action) occurs because of:
A. Surface tension and adhesion between liquid and tube walls
B. Gravity pulling liquid up
C. Atmospheric pressure pushing liquid up
D. Viscosity alone
Answer: A
Explanation: Adhesion > cohesion → liquid wets tube → meniscus curves → surface tension pulls liquid up. Exception: Mercury in glass: cohesion > adhesion → convex meniscus → mercury is depressed in capillary tube.

135. Viscosity is a measure of:
A. Internal resistance of a fluid to flow
B. Density of the fluid
C. Surface tension of the fluid
D. Compressibility of the fluid
Answer: A
Explanation: Higher viscosity = "thicker" fluid. Stokes' Law: drag force on sphere = 6πηrv. Exception: Viscosity of liquids decreases with temperature; gases increase with temperature — opposite trends.

136. Stokes' Law gives the drag force on a sphere as:
A. F = 6πηrv (terminal velocity when drag + upthrust = weight)
B. F = ηrv
C. F = 6πr²v
D. F = πηr²v
Answer: A
Explanation: At terminal velocity: 6πηrv + upthrust = mg. Exception: Stokes' Law applies only for low Reynolds number (laminar flow); for fast/large objects, turbulence occurs.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 14: OSCILLATIONS AND SHM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

137. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) requires:
A. Restoring force proportional to and directed toward equilibrium
B. Constant velocity
C. Zero friction
D. Circular motion
Answer: A
Explanation: F = -kx. Exception: SHM is an idealization; real oscillations are damped due to energy dissipation.

138. The velocity in SHM is maximum at:
A. The equilibrium position (center)
B. The amplitude positions
C. All positions equally
D. The half-amplitude position
Answer: A
Explanation: v = ω√(A² - x²); maximum when x = 0. Exception: At maximum displacement (x = A), velocity is zero; at x = 0, velocity is maximum (ωA).

139. The acceleration in SHM is maximum at:
A. Maximum displacement (x = ±A)
B. Equilibrium position
C. All positions equally
D. Quarter-amplitude position
Answer: A
Explanation: a = -ω²x; maximum |a| when |x| is maximum. Exception: At equilibrium, acceleration is zero — this is where velocity is maximum (they are 90° out of phase).

140. The period of a mass-spring system is:
A. T = 2π√(m/k)
B. T = 2π√(k/m)
C. T = 2π√(m/g)
D. T = 2π√(L/g)
Answer: A
Explanation: Stiffer spring (larger k) → shorter period. More mass → longer period. Exception: Period is independent of amplitude (for ideal SHM), just like the simple pendulum.

141. Total energy in SHM is:
A. E = ½kA² = ½mω²A² (constant, proportional to amplitude squared)
B. E = ½mv²
C. E = mgh
D. E varies with position
Answer: A
Explanation: KE + PE = constant = ½kA². Exception: Energy varies between KE and PE but total is constant (no damping).

SECTION 15: MODERN PHYSICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

142. de Broglie's hypothesis states:
A. Moving particles have an associated wavelength λ = h/mv
B. Light is only a particle
C. Electrons travel in straight lines
D. Mass and energy are equivalent
Answer: A
Explanation: Wave-particle duality of matter. Exception: For macroscopic objects, λ is incredibly small (immeasurable); observable only for electrons, atoms.

143. The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle states:
A. Cannot simultaneously know exact position AND momentum: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π
B. Energy is always uncertain
C. Electrons have no definite position at any time
D. Measurement always disturbs velocity only
Answer: A
Explanation: Fundamental limit — not a technological limitation. Exception: The more precisely you know position, the less precisely you know momentum, and vice versa.

144. In the photoelectric effect, increasing intensity of light:
A. Increases number of emitted electrons (not their maximum KE)
B. Increases maximum KE of electrons
C. Lowers the threshold frequency
D. Has no effect at all
Answer: A
Explanation: Intensity = number of photons/s. More photons → more electrons emitted. Exception: KE of each electron depends only on frequency (hf - φ), not intensity — intensity affects number, not energy.

145. The energy of a photon is:
A. E = hf = hc/λ
B. E = mc²
C. E = ½mv²
D. E = hλ
Answer: A
Explanation: h = Planck's constant = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. Exception: Photons have zero rest mass but carry energy and momentum (p = h/λ).

146. An electron volt (eV) is:
A. Energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 volt potential difference (1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)
B. Unit of power
C. Unit of charge
D. Energy of one photon
Answer: A
Explanation: Useful unit at atomic scale. Exception: 1 MeV = 10⁶ eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ J; used in nuclear physics.

147. The Bohr model of hydrogen explains:
A. Discrete spectral lines due to electrons jumping between fixed energy levels
B. Continuous emission spectra
C. Why all elements emit the same spectrum
D. Electron probability clouds
Answer: A
Explanation: Electrons emit/absorb photons when changing levels: E = hf = E₁ - E₂. Exception: Bohr model works well for hydrogen but fails for multi-electron atoms — quantum mechanics needed.

148. Which series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the ultraviolet region?
A. Lyman series (transitions to n=1)
B. Balmer series (to n=2)
C. Paschen series (to n=3)
D. Brackett series (to n=4)
Answer: A
Explanation: Lyman: largest energy transitions → UV. Balmer: visible. Paschen/Brackett: infrared. Exception: Balmer series was first discovered because visible light is easiest to detect.

149. Semiconductors have conductivity:
A. Between conductors and insulators, increasing with temperature
B. Lower than insulators
C. Constant regardless of temperature
D. Decreasing with temperature (like metals)
Answer: A
Explanation: As temperature rises, more electrons gain enough energy to jump the band gap. Exception: This is opposite to metals — semiconductor resistance decreases with temperature; metal resistance increases.

150. A p-n junction diode allows current to flow:
A. Mainly in one direction (forward bias only)
B. Both directions equally
C. Neither direction
D. Only when in reverse bias
Answer: A
Explanation: Forward bias reduces depletion region; reverse bias widens it. Exception: In reverse bias, a very small leakage current flows; at breakdown voltage, large current flows (used in Zener diodes).

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SECTION 16: ADDITIONAL HIGH-PROBABILITY TOPICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

151. Velocity-time graph: area under the graph represents:
A. Displacement
B. Acceleration
C. Distance per unit time
D. Force applied
Answer: A
Explanation: Area = v × t = displacement. Exception: For non-uniform velocity, the area is calculated by integration; for uniform sections, use simple geometric shapes.

152. Displacement-time graph: gradient (slope) represents:
A. Velocity
B. Acceleration
C. Distance
D. Speed only for straight-line motion
Answer: A
Explanation: Gradient = Δs/Δt = velocity. Exception: For a curved s-t graph, the instantaneous velocity = tangent slope; average velocity = chord slope.

153. An object is dropped from rest. Distance fallen in time t is:
A. s = ½gt²
B. s = gt
C. s = gt²
D. s = ½gt
Answer: A
Explanation: u = 0, a = g, s = ut + ½at² = ½gt². Exception: Air resistance reduces acceleration; at terminal velocity, s = vt (constant speed).

154. The equations of motion (SUVAT) apply when:
A. Acceleration is uniform (constant)
B. Any motion
C. Circular motion
D. Oscillatory motion
Answer: A
Explanation: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; s = ½(u+v)t. Exception: For variable acceleration, calculus (integration) must be used.

155. Impulse is equal to:
A. Change in momentum (Ft = Δ(mv))
B. Force × distance
C. Mass × acceleration
D. Work done
Answer: A
Explanation: Impulse = Ft = mv - mu. Exception: For variable force, impulse = ∫F dt (area under F-t graph) — the force-time graph is used in collision analysis.

156. Elastic collisions conserve:
A. Both kinetic energy and momentum
B. Momentum only
C. Kinetic energy only
D. Total energy only
Answer: A
Explanation: Elastic: no deformation, no energy loss. Exception: Perfectly elastic collisions are ideal; real collisions are somewhere between perfectly elastic and perfectly inelastic.

157. The angle of projection for maximum horizontal range is:
A. 45°
B. 30°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: A
Explanation: Range R = u²sin2θ/g; maximum when sin2θ = 1, i.e., θ = 45°. Exception: 30° and 60° give the same range (complementary angles); 90° gives zero horizontal range.

158. A body thrown upward with velocity u reaches maximum height:
A. H = u²/(2g)
B. H = u/g
C. H = u²/g
D. H = 2u²/g
Answer: A
Explanation: v² = u² - 2gH; at max height v = 0, so H = u²/2g. Exception: Time to reach max height = u/g; total flight time = 2u/g (same time up and down).

159. Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles. The resultant is:
A. 5 N (Pythagoras: 3² + 4² = 5²)
B. 7 N
C. 1 N
D. 12 N
Answer: A
Explanation: Vector addition at 90°: R = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 N. Exception: If forces are parallel (same direction), R = 7 N; opposite, R = 1 N. The angle matters!

160. Torque (moment of force) is:
A. Force × perpendicular distance from pivot
B. Force / distance
C. Force × displacement
D. Work / time
Answer: A
Explanation: τ = Fd (or rFsinθ for non-perpendicular). Exception: Torque and work have the same units (N·m = J) but are different concepts; torque causes rotation, work transfers energy.

161. The principle of moments states:
A. Sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments (for equilibrium)
B. Total force = zero
C. Moments always cancel
D. Forces act in pairs
Answer: A
Explanation: For a balanced lever or beam. Exception: This is the rotational equilibrium condition; translational equilibrium also requires ΣF = 0.

162. A uniform beam is balanced at its midpoint because:
A. Its weight acts at the center of gravity (which is at the midpoint for uniform beam)
B. Both halves have equal lengths
C. Moments automatically cancel
D. External forces act at midpoint
Answer: A
Explanation: Center of gravity of a uniform object is at its geometric center. Exception: Non-uniform beams (e.g., varying density) have center of gravity offset from midpoint.

163. Gravitational potential energy (GPE) is:
A. mgh (near Earth's surface)
B. ½mv²
C. Gm₁m₂/r
D. -Gm₁m₂/r
Answer: A
Explanation: GPE = mgh for objects near Earth where g is approximately constant. Exception: For objects far from Earth: GPE = -GMm/r (negative, with zero at infinity).

164. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:
A. F = Gm₁m₂/r²
B. F = Gm₁m₂/r
C. F = Gm₁m₂r²
D. F = g(m₁ + m₂)/r²
Answer: A
Explanation: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Exception: Gravity follows inverse square law — doubling distance reduces force by factor of 4, not 2.

165. The acceleration due to gravity on the Moon is about:
A. g_Moon ≈ 1.6 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's)
B. g_Moon = 0
C. g_Moon = 9.8 m/s² (same as Earth)
D. g_Moon = 3.2 m/s²
Answer: A
Explanation: Moon has less mass and smaller radius than Earth. Exception: An astronaut on Moon has same mass but weighs only 1/6 — mass is invariant, weight depends on gravitational field.

166. A body in free fall experiences:
A. Weightlessness (apparent weight = zero)
B. Increased weight
C. Normal weight
D. Doubled weight
Answer: A
Explanation: In free fall, reaction force from support = 0; apparent weight = m(g - a) = 0 when a = g. Exception: Weightlessness also occurs in orbit — astronauts are in continuous free fall around Earth.

167. The velocity of sound in air at 0°C is approximately:
A. 330 m/s
B. 3 × 10⁸ m/s
C. 1500 m/s
D. 100 m/s
Answer: A
Explanation: v_sound ≈ 330 m/s in air at 0°C, ~340 m/s at 20°C. Exception: Sound travels faster in liquids (~1500 m/s in water) and even faster in solids (~5000 m/s in steel) — denser medium, faster sound (for solids and liquids).

168. The decibel scale of sound intensity:
A. Is logarithmic; 10 dB increase means 10× intensity increase
B. Is linear
C. 10 dB increase means 10 dB more amplitude
D. Is based on frequency
Answer: A
Explanation: dB = 10 log(I/I₀). Exception: 3 dB increase ≈ doubles intensity; 10 dB increase = 10× intensity; 20 dB = 100× intensity.

169. Light travels at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in:
A. Vacuum only (slower in any medium)
B. All transparent materials
C. Air and vacuum equally
D. Glass faster than in air
Answer: A
Explanation: In any medium, v = c/n < c. Exception: Extremely energetic particles in dense media can exceed the speed of light in that medium — producing Cherenkov radiation (the blue glow in nuclear reactors).

170. The frequency of light does NOT change when it:
A. Passes from one medium to another (wavelength and speed change, not frequency)
B. Reflects off a mirror
C. Passes through a prism
D. All of the above (frequency never changes in any of these)
Answer: D
Explanation: Frequency is fixed by the source. In a new medium, v and λ change proportionally (v = fλ). Exception: The Doppler effect CAN change observed frequency if source/observer moves relative to each other.

171. When two identical waves meet in phase:
A. Constructive interference occurs; amplitude doubles
B. Destructive interference; amplitude = 0
C. No interference
D. Frequency doubles
Answer: A
Explanation: Path difference = nλ for constructive; (n + ½)λ for destructive. Exception: Destructive interference doesn't destroy energy — it redistributes it to other locations.

172. The critical angle (C) and refractive index (n) are related by:
A. sinC = 1/n
B. sinC = n
C. cosC = 1/n
D. tanC = 1/n
Answer: A
Explanation: At critical angle, refracted ray is at 90°: n₁sinC = n₂sin90° → sinC = n₂/n₁ = 1/n (going from glass to air). Exception: Critical angle only exists for denser-to-rarer transitions.

173. A convex mirror always produces an image that is:
A. Virtual, erect, and diminished regardless of object position
B. Real and inverted for objects beyond F
C. Magnified for close objects
D. Real and erect
Answer: A
Explanation: Convex mirror: f is negative (or behind mirror); all images are virtual, erect, and smaller. Exception: This makes convex mirrors useful as rear-view and security mirrors despite image being smaller.

174. The magnification of a simple microscope (magnifying glass) is:
A. M = 1 + D/f (D = near point distance, usually 25 cm)
B. M = D/f
C. M = f/D
D. M = D × f
Answer: A
Explanation: Image formed at near point (25 cm). Exception: M = D/f when image formed at infinity (relaxed eye); both formulae appear in JAMB.

175. An astronomical telescope in normal adjustment:
A. Has objective and eyepiece separated by f_o + f_e (image at infinity)
B. Has them separated by f_o - f_e
C. Forms an image between the lenses
D. Uses a concave objective
Answer: A
Explanation: Normal adjustment: final image at infinity (for relaxed eye viewing). Exception: Magnification = f_o/f_e; large objective focal length and small eyepiece focal length gives more magnification.

176. The quantity of charge is measured in:
A. Coulombs (C)
B. Amperes
C. Volts
D. Joules
Answer: A
Explanation: Q = It; 1 Coulomb = 1 Ampere × 1 second. Exception: Charge of an electron = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; one Coulomb is about 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons.

177. A thermistor is a resistor whose resistance:
A. Decreases with increasing temperature (NTC type)
B. Increases with increasing temperature
C. Is independent of temperature
D. Only changes with light intensity
Answer: A
Explanation: NTC (Negative Temperature Coefficient) thermistors are most common; used in thermostats and temperature sensors. Exception: PTC (Positive Temperature Coefficient) thermistors also exist and DO increase resistance with temperature.

178. A light-dependent resistor (LDR) has resistance that:
A. Decreases in bright light (more light → more current)
B. Increases in bright light
C. Is constant regardless of light
D. Depends on wavelength only
Answer: A
Explanation: More photons → more charge carriers → lower resistance. Exception: LDRs respond to light intensity, not frequency; used in automatic street lights and camera exposure controls.

179. In series circuit, the component with highest resistance:
A. Has the greatest voltage across it (V = IR, same I)
B. Has the most current through it
C. Dissipates least power
D. Has lowest voltage across it
Answer: A
Explanation: In series: current is same; voltage divides proportionally to resistance. Exception: Power P = I²R — higher resistance also means higher power dissipation in series (same current).

180. A moving-coil galvanometer can be converted to an ammeter by:
A. Adding a low-resistance shunt in parallel
B. Adding a high resistance in series
C. Adding a high resistance in parallel
D. Adding a low resistance in series
Answer: A
Explanation: Shunt diverts most current; only a small fraction goes through the galvanometer. Exception: Voltmeter = high resistance in series (limits current; reads voltage by V = IR).

181. The Earth's magnetic field is approximately:
A. 5 × 10⁻⁵ T (varies by location)
B. 1 T
C. 10⁻¹⁰ T
D. 1 mT
Answer: A
Explanation: Earth's field is weak (~0.05 mT) but sufficient to deflect compass needles and charged particles. Exception: MRI machines use fields of 1.5–3 T; Earth's field is 100,000 times weaker.

182. Eddy currents are:
A. Induced currents circulating in bulk conductors due to changing magnetic flux
B. Currents flowing along edges of conductors
C. Currents in AC circuits
D. Magnetic field lines in conductors
Answer: A
Explanation: Eddy currents dissipate energy as heat (Lenz's Law). Exception: Eddy currents are useful in induction cooktops and electromagnetic braking; undesirable in transformer cores (reduced by lamination).

183. A Fleming's left-hand rule predicts:
A. Direction of force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field (motor effect)
B. Direction of induced current in a generator
C. Direction of electron flow
D. Polarity of an electromagnet
Answer: A
Explanation: Left hand: thuMb = Motion (force), Index = field, Middle = current. Exception: Fleming's right-hand rule is for generators — easy to mix them up in exams.

184. Self-induction occurs when:
A. A changing current in a coil induces EMF in the same coil
B. One coil induces EMF in another coil
C. A conductor moves in a magnetic field
D. A capacitor charges
Answer: A
Explanation: L·(dI/dt) = induced EMF. Exception: Self-induction opposes changes in current — inductors act like "electrical inertia," resisting sudden changes.

185. The unit of inductance is:
A. Henry (H)
B. Farad (F)
C. Tesla (T)
D. Weber (Wb)
Answer: A
Explanation: 1 H = 1 V·s/A. Exception: Practical inductors are mH (millihenry) or μH; large inductances require many turns and ferromagnetic cores.

186. Magnetic flux (Φ) is defined as:
A. Φ = BA·cosθ (B = field, A = area, θ = angle between B and normal to area)
B. Φ = B/A
C. Φ = BA·sinθ
D. Φ = B·A²
Answer: A
Explanation: Unit: Weber (Wb = T·m²). Exception: Maximum flux when B is perpendicular to the surface (θ = 0, cosθ = 1); zero flux when B is parallel to surface (θ = 90°).

187. A diode in a circuit acts as:
A. One-way valve for current (rectifier)
B. Amplifier
C. Resistance that changes with voltage
D. A transformer
Answer: A
Explanation: Forward biased: low resistance, conducts. Reverse biased: high resistance, blocks. Exception: Zener diodes are designed to operate in reverse breakdown — used as voltage regulators.

188. A transistor can be used as:
A. Both a switch and an amplifier
B. Only a switch
C. Only an amplifier
D. A diode only
Answer: A
Explanation: Small base current controls larger collector current. Exception: In digital electronics, transistors switch (on/off); in analog electronics, they amplify continuously varying signals.

189. The speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum is:
A. c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s for ALL EM waves regardless of frequency
B. Depends on frequency
C. Depends on wavelength
D. Depends on amplitude
Answer: A
Explanation: All EM waves travel at c in vacuum. Exception: Speed changes in media (v = c/n), but frequency remains the same — wavelength changes instead.

190. The wavelength of visible light ranges approximately:
A. 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red)
B. 1 nm to 100 nm
C. 1 mm to 1 m
D. 100 nm to 400 nm
Answer: A
Explanation: Visible spectrum occupies a narrow range. Exception: Human eye is most sensitive to green (~550 nm); different organisms see different ranges (e.g., bees see UV).

191. When a gas is compressed isothermally, its temperature:
A. Remains constant (definition of isothermal)
B. Increases
C. Decreases
D. Changes unpredictably
Answer: A
Explanation: Isothermal: ΔT = 0, so PV = constant (Boyle's Law). Exception: For compression to be truly isothermal, heat must be removed from the gas very slowly.

192. In an adiabatic process:
A. No heat exchange with surroundings (Q = 0)
B. Temperature is constant
C. Pressure is constant
D. Volume is constant
Answer: A
Explanation: ΔU = -W (first law with Q = 0); compression raises temperature; expansion lowers temperature. Exception: Sudden compressions (like in a diesel engine) are approximately adiabatic because they happen too fast for heat to escape.

193. Radiation from the Sun that reaches Earth primarily consists of:
A. Visible light and near-infrared (with some UV)
B. X-rays and gamma rays
C. Only visible light
D. Mostly radio waves
Answer: A
Explanation: Sun's surface temperature ~5500°C → peak emission in visible/near-IR range. Exception: Atmosphere filters out most UV and all X/gamma rays; satellite instruments detect the full spectrum.

194. A body on a frictionless inclined plane accelerates at:
A. g·sinθ (component of gravity along the plane)
B. g
C. g·cosθ
D. g·tanθ
Answer: A
Explanation: Net force along plane = mg·sinθ; a = g·sinθ. Exception: Normal force = mg·cosθ — the component perpendicular to the incline (does no work on the block).

195. Pressure in a hydraulic system: applying force F₁ on area A₁ produces force F₂ on area A₂ where:
A. F₂/F₁ = A₂/A₁ (Pascal's principle)
B. F₂ = F₁ always
C. F₂ = F₁ × A₁ × A₂
D. F₂/F₁ = A₁/A₂
Answer: A
Explanation: Pressure transmitted equally: P = F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂. Exception: While force is multiplied, displacement is reduced (energy conservation); small piston moves farther than large piston.

196. The binding energy per nucleon is greatest for:
A. Iron-56 (most stable nucleus)
B. Hydrogen
C. Uranium-235
D. Carbon-12
Answer: A
Explanation: Iron-56 sits at the peak of binding energy per nucleon curve (~8.8 MeV). Exception: Fusion of elements lighter than Fe releases energy; fission of elements heavier than Fe releases energy — both "move toward" iron.

197. A nuclear equation must balance:
A. Both mass number (A) and atomic number (Z)
B. Only mass number
C. Only atomic number
D. Number of neutrons only
Answer: A
Explanation: Conservation of nucleons and charge. Exception: Energy (E = Δmc²) is also released; the mass defect is the source of nuclear energy.

198. Background radiation comes from:
A. Cosmic rays, naturally occurring radioactive materials, and radon gas (always present)
B. Nuclear power stations only
C. X-ray machines only
D. Artificial sources only
Answer: A
Explanation: Background radiation is always present; all measurements must subtract it. Exception: Radon gas (from ground) is the largest source of background radiation in many countries.

199. The activity of a radioactive sample is:
A. Number of decays per second (measured in Becquerel, Bq)
B. Energy released per decay
C. Number of nuclei remaining
D. Rate of change of mass
Answer: A
Explanation: A = λN (activity = decay constant × number of undecayed nuclei). Exception: Activity and number of nuclei both follow exponential decay: A = A₀·e^(-λt) and N = N₀·e^(-λt).

200. Pair production occurs when:
A. A high-energy photon produces an electron-positron pair (near a nucleus)
B. Two electrons collide
C. A proton captures a neutron
D. A nucleus emits alpha and beta simultaneously
Answer: A
Explanation: γ → e⁻ + e⁺; requires photon energy ≥ 2m_e·c² ≈ 1.02 MeV; nucleus needed to conserve momentum. Exception: The reverse process — pair annihilation — produces two 0.511 MeV gamma photons; used in PET scans.
