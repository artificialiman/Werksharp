SECTION A: ATOMIC STRUCTURE & PERIODICITY (Q1–25)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. An atom of element X has 19 protons and 20 neutrons. Its mass number is:
A. 39
B. 19
C. 20
D. 38
Answer: A
Explanation: Mass number = protons + neutrons = 19 + 20 = 39. This is potassium-39 (⁳⁹K).

2. Isotopes of an element have the same number of:
A. Protons only
B. Neutrons only
C. Protons and neutrons
D. Mass number
Answer: A
Explanation: Isotopes = same atomic number (protons) but different neutron count, hence different mass numbers.

3. The atomic number of an element is 16. Its electronic configuration is:
A. 2, 8, 6
B. 2, 6, 8
C. 8, 8
D. 2, 14
Answer: A
Explanation: Electrons fill shells: K=2, L=8, M=6. This is sulfur.

4. Which particle has the smallest mass?
A. Electron
B. Proton
C. Neutron
D. Alpha particle
Answer: A
Explanation: Electron mass ≈ 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, roughly 1/1836 of a proton. Alpha particles are 4 amu.

5. The atomic radius generally decreases across a period because:
A. Increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer
B. More electron shells are added
C. Shielding effect increases
D. Electronegativity decreases
Answer: A
Explanation: Across a period, proton number increases while shell number stays the same—greater nuclear pull contracts electron cloud.

6. Which element has the highest electronegativity?
A. Fluorine
B. Oxygen
C. Chlorine
D. Nitrogen
Answer: A
Explanation: Fluorine (3.98 on Pauling scale) — highest electronegativity of all elements. Electronegativity increases up and across the periodic table.

7. The first ionization energy of aluminium is lower than magnesium because:
A. Al's outer electron is in a 3p subshell, easier to remove than 3s
B. Al has more electrons
C. Al is larger
D. Al has lower nuclear charge
Answer: A
Explanation: Mg: [Ne]3s². Al: [Ne]3s²3p¹. The 3p electron is higher in energy and better shielded than the 3s — classic UTME exception question.

8. Which of these pairs are isoelectronic?
A. Na⁺ and Ne
B. Na⁺ and K⁺
C. Cl⁻ and Na⁺
D. O²⁻ and N³⁻
Answer: A
Explanation: Na⁺ loses 1 electron → 10 electrons. Ne has 10 electrons. Isoelectronic = same number of electrons.

9. An element in Period 3 and Group VIA has atomic number:
A. 16
B. 14
C. 18
D. 13
Answer: A
Explanation: Period 3 Group VIA = sulfur, atomic number 16.

10. Which of the following has the largest ionic radius?
A. O²⁻
B. F⁻
C. Na⁺
D. Mg²⁺
Answer: A
Explanation: All isoelectronic (10 electrons), but O²⁻ has fewest protons (8), so least nuclear pull — largest radius.

11. The energy of an electron in the hydrogen atom is given by E = -1312/n² kJ/mol. The energy of the n=2 level is:
A. -328 kJ/mol
B. -656 kJ/mol
C. -1312 kJ/mol
D. -164 kJ/mol
Answer: A
Explanation: E = -1312/4 = -328 kJ/mol. Energy becomes less negative (higher/closer to zero) at higher n.

12. Which quantum number describes the shape of an orbital?
A. Azimuthal (l)
B. Principal (n)
C. Magnetic (m)
D. Spin (s)
Answer: A
Explanation: l=0 is s (spherical), l=1 is p (dumbbell), l=2 is d. n describes energy level; m describes orientation.

13. The maximum number of electrons in the d subshell is:
A. 10
B. 6
C. 2
D. 14
Answer: A
Explanation: d has 5 orbitals × 2 electrons each = 10. s=2, p=6, d=10, f=14.

14. Across Period 3, which property decreases?
A. Atomic radius
B. Ionization energy
C. Electronegativity
D. Nuclear charge
Answer: A
Explanation: Atomic radius decreases left to right as nuclear charge increases. All others increase across a period.

15. The element with configuration [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ is:
A. Chromium
B. Manganese
C. Iron
D. Vanadium
Answer: A
Explanation: Cr (Z=24) adopts [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ rather than 3d⁴4s² — half-filled d subshell is extra stable. Classic UTME exception.

16. How many unpaired electrons does oxygen have?
A. 2
B. 1
C. 0
D. 3
Answer: A
Explanation: O: 1s²2s²2p⁴. The 2p subshell has one fully filled, two with one electron each — 2 unpaired electrons.

17. The electron affinity of chlorine is greater than that of fluorine because:
A. Cl has a larger atomic radius, less electron-electron repulsion when gaining an electron
B. Cl is more electronegative
C. Cl has fewer electrons
D. Cl is in Period 3
Answer: A
Explanation: F is so small that electron-electron repulsion in the already-crowded 2p orbitals reduces energy released. Cl accommodates the extra electron more easily.

18. The ionic compound with the highest lattice energy among these would be:
A. MgO
B. NaCl
C. KI
D. LiF
Answer: A
Explanation: Lattice energy ∝ charge²/radius. Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ both doubly charged and small → highest lattice energy.

19. Which of the following elements is a metalloid?
A. Silicon
B. Phosphorus
C. Sulfur
D. Calcium
Answer: A
Explanation: Silicon (Si) has properties between metals and non-metals. Metalloids: B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te.

20. Dobereiner's triads grouped elements in sets of three with the middle element's atomic mass being:
A. The average of the other two
B. The sum of the other two
C. Half the lightest element
D. Equal to the heaviest
Answer: A
Explanation: E.g. Li (7), Na (23), K (39): Na ≈ (7+39)/2 = 23. Early precursor to periodic law.

21. Mendeleev arranged elements by increasing:
A. Atomic mass
B. Atomic number
C. Electronegativity
D. Atomic radius
Answer: A
Explanation: Mendeleev used atomic mass (1869). Moseley later corrected this to atomic number (1913), resolving anomalies like Te/I.

22. The modern periodic law states that properties of elements are periodic functions of their:
A. Atomic number
B. Atomic mass
C. Number of neutrons
D. Valence electrons
Answer: A
Explanation: Moseley's periodic law: properties repeat with increasing atomic number, not mass.

23. The valency of an element with electronic configuration 2, 8, 7 is:
A. 1
B. 7
C. 3
D. 2
Answer: A
Explanation: 7 valence electrons — needs 1 more to complete octet, so valency = 1. This is chlorine.

24. Which of the following elements forms an oxide with formula X₂O₅?
A. Nitrogen
B. Carbon
C. Sulfur
D. Phosphorus (P₂O₅ not N₂O₅)
Answer: A
Explanation: Nitrogen forms N₂O₅ (Group V element, +5 oxidation state). Note: P also forms P₂O₅.

25. A transition element is one that has an incomplete:
A. d subshell in at least one oxidation state
B. p subshell
C. s subshell
D. f subshell
Answer: A
Explanation: Definition: element with partially filled d subshell in elemental form or common ions. Zn and Sc are borderline cases by this definition.

SECTION B: CHEMICAL BONDING (Q26–50)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

26. The bond in NaCl is:
A. Ionic (electron transfer)
B. Covalent
C. Metallic
D. Dative
Answer: A
Explanation: Na (metal) loses 1 electron to Cl (non-metal). Electronegativity difference > 1.7 indicates ionic character.

27. Which molecule has a tetrahedral shape?
A. CH₄
B. H₂O
C. NH₃
D. CO₂
Answer: A
Explanation: CH₄: 4 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs → perfect tetrahedral (109.5°). NH₃ is trigonal pyramidal; H₂O is bent.

28. The shape of NH₃ is:
A. Trigonal pyramidal
B. Trigonal planar
C. Tetrahedral
D. Linear
Answer: A
Explanation: N has 3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair. VSEPR: lone pair pushes bonds → trigonal pyramidal, 107°.

29. Which of these has the highest boiling point due to hydrogen bonding?
A. H₂O
B. H₂S
C. HCl
D. CH₄
Answer: A
Explanation: H₂O has extensive H-bonding (O–H···O). This network raises bp far above Group 16 trend.

30. A dative (coordinate) bond is formed when:
A. One atom donates both electrons to the bond
B. Each atom donates one electron
C. Electrons are transferred completely
D. Ions attract each other
Answer: A
Explanation: Also called coordinate bond. Example: NH₄⁺ (N donates lone pair to H⁺), H₃O⁺, [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺.

31. The bond angle in CO₂ is:
A. 180°
B. 120°
C. 109.5°
D. 104.5°
Answer: A
Explanation: CO₂ is linear (sp hybridization). Two double bonds, no lone pairs on C → 180°.

32. Which has the shortest bond length?
A. N≡N (triple bond)
B. N=N (double bond)
C. N–N (single bond)
D. All equal
Answer: A
Explanation: Bond length decreases as bond order increases. Triple bond: shorter and stronger than double, which is shorter than single.

33. Metallic bonding involves:
A. A sea of delocalized electrons surrounding positive ions
B. Sharing of electrons between two atoms
C. Transfer of electrons
D. Electrostatic attraction of ions
Answer: A
Explanation: Explains metals' conductivity, malleability, ductility. Delocalized electrons carry charge and allow layers to slide.

34. Which type of solid has the highest melting point?
A. Network covalent (e.g. diamond, SiO₂)
B. Ionic
C. Metallic
D. Molecular
Answer: A
Explanation: Covalent network solids require breaking many strong covalent bonds. Diamond mp > 3500°C.

35. The hybridization of nitrogen in NH₃ is:
A. sp³
B. sp²
C. sp
D. sp³d
Answer: A
Explanation: N has 4 electron groups (3 bonds + 1 lone pair) → sp³. Bond angle 107° (slightly compressed by lone pair).

36. Van der Waals forces are strongest in:
A. I₂ (heaviest, most electrons)
B. F₂
C. Cl₂
D. Br₂
Answer: A
Explanation: Dispersion forces ∝ number of electrons (polarizability). I₂ has most electrons → strongest temporary dipoles → highest bp among halogens.

37. Which molecule is polar?
A. HCl
B. CO₂
C. CCl₄
D. BF₃
Answer: A
Explanation: HCl: different atoms, charge separation, linear but unsymmetric → dipole moment. CO₂, CCl₄, BF₃ have symmetric geometry cancelling dipoles.

38. The bond in H₂ is described as:
A. Nonpolar covalent
B. Polar covalent
C. Ionic
D. Dative
Answer: A
Explanation: H–H: identical atoms → equal electron sharing → zero electronegativity difference → nonpolar covalent.

39. Graphite conducts electricity because:
A. It has delocalized electrons in its layered structure
B. It is a metal
C. It has ionic bonds
D. It contains free ions
Answer: A
Explanation: Each C in graphite is sp² hybridized. The unhybridized p electrons form a delocalized π system across each layer.

40. Which species has a lone pair of electrons acting as a Lewis base?
A. NH₃
B. BF₃
C. AlCl₃
D. H⁺
Answer: A
Explanation: Lewis base = electron pair donor. NH₃ donates via N lone pair. BF₃ and AlCl₃ are Lewis acids (electron acceptors).

41. The number of sigma (σ) bonds in ethene (C₂H₄) is:
A. 5
B. 4
C. 6
D. 3
Answer: A
Explanation: C=C (1σ + 1π), each C–H (1σ each × 4) → 1 + 4 = 5 sigma bonds total.

42. A sigma bond is formed by:
A. End-to-end (head-on) orbital overlap
B. Side-to-side (lateral) orbital overlap
C. Electron transfer
D. Ion-dipole attraction
Answer: A
Explanation: Sigma = end-on overlap (stronger, can rotate freely). Pi = lateral overlap (weaker, restricts rotation — explains geometric isomerism in alkenes).

43. The bond angle in BF₃ is:
A. 120°
B. 109.5°
C. 107°
D. 90°
Answer: A
Explanation: BF₃: 3 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs → trigonal planar → 120°. B is sp² hybridized and electron deficient.

44. Which of these has the strongest intermolecular forces?
A. Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)
B. Ethane (C₂H₆)
C. Propane (C₃H₈)
D. Diethyl ether
Answer: A
Explanation: Ethanol has O–H group → hydrogen bonding. All others rely only on weaker van der Waals/dipole forces.

45. How many lone pairs does the central atom in H₂O have?
A. 2
B. 1
C. 0
D. 3
Answer: A
Explanation: O: 6 valence electrons. 2 used in O–H bonds (2 bonding pairs). Remaining 4 electrons = 2 lone pairs.

46. The octet rule is violated in:
A. PCl₅
B. CCl₄
C. NaCl
D. NH₃
Answer: A
Explanation: PCl₅: P has 5 bonds = 10 electrons around P (expanded octet, uses 3d orbitals). Period 3+ elements can exceed octet.

47. The lattice energy of an ionic compound is the energy required to:
A. Separate 1 mole of ionic solid into gaseous ions
B. Dissolve the compound in water
C. Form the compound from elements
D. Melt the compound
Answer: A
Explanation: Lattice energy = energy to completely separate ionic lattice into gaseous ions. Higher lattice energy = more stable compound.

48. Which of these compounds exhibits the most covalent character?
A. AlCl₃
B. NaCl
C. MgCl₂
D. KCl
Answer: A
Explanation: Fajans' rules: high charge density cation + large, polarizable anion → covalent character. Al³⁺ has highest charge density here.

49. Resonance in organic compounds results in:
A. Greater stability than any single Lewis structure predicts
B. Alternating single and double bonds
C. Different compounds in equilibrium
D. Weaker bonds
Answer: A
Explanation: Delocalization of electrons lowers energy. Benzene's C–C bonds are all equal length (1.40 Å) between single (1.54) and double (1.34).

50. In a hydrogen bond, the hydrogen atom is covalently bonded to:
A. A small, highly electronegative atom (F, O, or N)
B. Any electronegative atom
C. Carbon
D. A metal
Answer: A
Explanation: Only F, O, N are electronegative/small enough to create the necessary partial positive on H and provide lone pairs for H-bond acceptors.

SECTION C: STOICHIOMETRY & MOLE CONCEPT (Q51–70)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

51. The molar mass of CaCO₃ is:
A. 100 g/mol
B. 80 g/mol
C. 120 g/mol
D. 98 g/mol
Answer: A
Explanation: Ca(40) + C(12) + 3×O(16) = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100 g/mol.

52. Avogadro's number (6.02×10²³) represents the number of particles in:
A. 1 mole of any substance
B. 1 gram of any substance
C. 1 litre of gas at STP
D. 1 molecule
Answer: A
Explanation: 1 mole = 6.02×10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, ions, etc.) regardless of substance.

53. What volume does 1 mole of an ideal gas occupy at STP?
A. 22.4 L
B. 24.0 L
C. 22.0 L
D. 44.8 L
Answer: A
Explanation: STP (0°C, 1 atm): molar volume = 22.4 L. At RTP (25°C, 1 atm): 24.0 L. UTME uses 22.4 L unless stated otherwise.

54. How many moles are in 11 g of CO₂? (M = 44 g/mol)
A. 0.25 mol
B. 0.5 mol
C. 1 mol
D. 2 mol
Answer: A
Explanation: n = mass/M = 11/44 = 0.25 mol.

55. The empirical formula of a compound containing 40% C, 6.7% H, and 53.3% O is:
A. CH₂O
B. C₂H₄O₂
C. CHO
D. C₂H₂O
Answer: A
Explanation: Divide by atomic masses: C=40/12=3.33, H=6.7/1=6.7, O=53.3/16=3.33. Ratio 1:2:1 → CH₂O.

56. In the reaction N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, how many moles of NH₃ are produced from 3 moles of H₂?
A. 2 mol
B. 3 mol
C. 1 mol
D. 6 mol
Answer: A
Explanation: 3H₂ → 2NH₃ directly from stoichiometry. 3 mol H₂ produces 2 mol NH₃.

57. The percentage by mass of nitrogen in NH₄NO₃ (M=80) is:
A. 35%
B. 28%
C. 17.5%
D. 14%
Answer: A
Explanation: 2 N atoms = 2×14 = 28. % = (28/80)×100 = 35%.

58. A limiting reagent is:
A. The reactant that is completely consumed first
B. The reactant in excess
C. The product of a reaction
D. A catalyst
Answer: A
Explanation: Limiting reagent determines maximum product. Excess reagent remains after reaction ends.

59. The concentration of a solution containing 4 g of NaOH (M=40) in 500 cm³ is:
A. 0.2 mol/L
B. 0.4 mol/L
C. 0.1 mol/L
D. 2 mol/L
Answer: A
Explanation: moles = 4/40 = 0.1 mol. Concentration = 0.1/0.5 L = 0.2 mol/L.

60. What mass of oxygen is needed to completely combust 16 g of CH₄? (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O)
A. 64 g
B. 32 g
C. 16 g
D. 48 g
Answer: A
Explanation: 16 g CH₄ = 1 mol. 1 mol CH₄ needs 2 mol O₂ = 2×32 = 64 g O₂.

61. Molarity is defined as:
A. Moles of solute per litre of solution
B. Moles of solute per kg of solvent
C. Mass of solute per litre
D. Volume of solute per volume of solution
Answer: A
Explanation: Molarity (M) = mol/L. Molality (m) = mol/kg solvent. Know both definitions.

62. The number of molecules in 22 g of CO₂ (M=44) is:
A. 3.01×10²³
B. 6.02×10²³
C. 1.20×10²⁴
D. 1.50×10²³
Answer: A
Explanation: 22/44 = 0.5 mol. 0.5 × 6.02×10²³ = 3.01×10²³ molecules.

63. Diluting a solution 10 times means the new concentration is:
A. 1/10 of original
B. 10 times original
C. Same
D. Square root of original
Answer: A
Explanation: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂. If V₂ = 10V₁, then C₂ = C₁/10. Dilution reduces concentration proportionally.

64. The equivalent mass of H₂SO₄ (M=98) in acid-base reactions is:
A. 49 g/equiv
B. 98 g/equiv
C. 24.5 g/equiv
D. 32.7 g/equiv
Answer: A
Explanation: Equivalent mass = M/n where n = number of H⁺ supplied = 2. Equiv. mass = 98/2 = 49.

65. Gay-Lussac's Law of combining volumes states:
A. Gases react and are produced in simple whole number volume ratios at constant T and P
B. Gases at same T and P have equal volumes
C. Volume is proportional to temperature
D. Equal volumes contain equal masses
Answer: A
Explanation: Basis for stoichiometry with gases. E.g., H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl (1:1:2 volumes).

66. A 25 cm³ sample of H₂SO₄ is neutralized by 50 cm³ of 0.1 M NaOH. The molarity of H₂SO₄ is:
A. 0.1 M
B. 0.2 M
C. 0.05 M
D. 0.5 M
Answer: A
Explanation: H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. mol NaOH = 0.05×0.1 = 0.005. mol H₂SO₄ = 0.005/2 = 0.0025. M = 0.0025/0.025 = 0.1 M.

67. The yield of a reaction where 5 g product is obtained from a theoretical yield of 20 g is:
A. 25%
B. 50%
C. 75%
D. 100%
Answer: A
Explanation: % yield = (actual/theoretical)×100 = (5/20)×100 = 25%.

68. At STP, how many moles of gas are in 44.8 L?
A. 2 mol
B. 1 mol
C. 0.5 mol
D. 4 mol
Answer: A
Explanation: 44.8 L ÷ 22.4 L/mol = 2 mol.

69. What is the formula mass of (NH₄)₂SO₄?
A. 132 g/mol
B. 96 g/mol
C. 114 g/mol
D. 148 g/mol
Answer: A
Explanation: 2×N(14)+8×H(1)+S(32)+4×O(16) = 28+8+32+64 = 132 g/mol.

70. The number of atoms in 1 mole of O₂ is:
A. 12.04×10²³
B. 6.02×10²³
C. 3.01×10²³
D. 1.00×10²³
Answer: A
Explanation: 1 mol O₂ = 6.02×10²³ molecules. Each O₂ has 2 atoms → 2 × 6.02×10²³ = 12.04×10²³ atoms.

SECTION D: ACIDS, BASES & SALTS (Q71–90)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

71. According to Arrhenius, an acid is a substance that:
A. Produces H⁺ ions in aqueous solution
B. Accepts protons
C. Donates electron pairs
D. Produces OH⁻ ions
Answer: A
Explanation: Arrhenius (1884): acid → H⁺, base → OH⁻ in water. Limited to aqueous solutions.

72. The Brønsted-Lowry conjugate base of H₂SO₄ is:
A. HSO₄⁻
B. SO₄²⁻
C. H₃SO₄⁺
D. H₂O
Answer: A
Explanation: Conjugate base = acid minus one proton. H₂SO₄ → H⁺ + HSO₄⁻. (Full deprotonation gives SO₄²⁻, which is the conjugate base of HSO₄⁻.)

73. A salt formed from a weak acid and strong base gives a solution that is:
A. Basic (pH > 7)
B. Acidic
C. Neutral
D. pH 7
Answer: A
Explanation: The anion of the weak acid (e.g. CH₃COO⁻) hydrolyzes water: CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O → CH₃COOH + OH⁻, producing excess OH⁻.

74. The pH of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
A. 7
B. 0
C. 14
D. Depends on concentration
Answer: A
Explanation: At 25°C, Kw = 10⁻¹⁴, so [H⁺]=[OH⁻]=10⁻⁷, pH = 7. This changes at different temperatures.

75. Which indicator would be most suitable for a strong acid-weak base titration?
A. Methyl orange (pH range 3.1–4.4)
B. Phenolphthalein
C. Litmus
D. Bromothymol blue
Answer: A
Explanation: Equivalence point is in acidic region (pH<7). Methyl orange (turns pink in acid) changes color at this region. Phenolphthalein is for acid-strong base titrations.

76. The reaction of an acid with a carbonate produces:
A. Salt + water + CO₂
B. Salt + water only
C. Salt + H₂ gas
D. Salt + CO only
Answer: A
Explanation: E.g. 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂. CO₂ causes effervescence — qualitative test for carbonates.

77. An amphoteric substance can act as:
A. Both an acid and a base
B. Both oxidizing and reducing agent
C. A catalyst
D. A buffer
Answer: A
Explanation: Examples: water (H₂O), aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃), amino acids. Al₂O₃ + HCl → AlCl₃; Al₂O₃ + NaOH → NaAlO₂.

78. The normality of H₃PO₄ solution that is 2 M is:
A. 6 N
B. 2 N
C. 4 N
D. 3 N
Answer: A
Explanation: H₃PO₄ has 3 replaceable H⁺ ions. Normality = Molarity × n-factor = 2 × 3 = 6 N.

79. When excess NaOH is added to ZnCl₂ solution, the precipitate formed:
A. Dissolves (Zn(OH)₂ is amphoteric)
B. Remains white
C. Turns blue
D. Remains insoluble
Answer: A
Explanation: Zn(OH)₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + 2H₂O. Zinc hydroxide dissolves in excess base — amphoteric character.

80. Hydrolysis of salt Na₂CO₃ produces a solution that turns litmus:
A. Blue (basic)
B. Red (acidic)
C. No change (neutral)
D. Purple
Answer: A
Explanation: Na₂CO₃ is a salt of strong base + weak acid. CO₃²⁻ hydrolyzes to give OH⁻ → basic solution.

81. The reaction between an acid and a base is called:
A. Neutralization
B. Oxidation
C. Reduction
D. Displacement
Answer: A
Explanation: Acid + base → salt + water (for Arrhenius). ΔH of neutralization of strong acid + strong base ≈ -57 kJ/mol.

82. Which acid is both a monobasic and a weak acid?
A. Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH)
B. H₂SO₄
C. HCl
D. H₃PO₄
Answer: A
Explanation: CH₃COOH has 1 ionizable proton (monobasic) and is only partially ionized (Ka = 1.8×10⁻⁵), hence weak.

83. The degree of ionization of a weak acid increases when:
A. The solution is diluted
B. Temperature decreases
C. More acid is added
D. A common ion is added
Answer: A
Explanation: Dilution shifts equilibrium toward ionization (Le Chatelier). Common ion effect (e.g. adding CH₃COO⁻) suppresses ionization.

84. Titration curve of strong acid vs strong base has a sharp inflection at:
A. pH 7
B. pH 4
C. pH 9
D. pH 11
Answer: A
Explanation: Equivalence point is at pH 7 for strong-strong titration. Weak acid-strong base: pH > 7; strong acid-weak base: pH < 7.

85. Which of the following is not a property of acids?
A. Turns litmus paper red
B. Reacts with metals to give H₂
C. Conducts electricity only
D. Has pH < 7
Answer: C
Explanation: Acids can conduct electricity (in solution), turn litmus red, pH<7, react with metals — but "conducts electricity only" is not a property of acids exclusively.

86. The acid found in bee stings is:
A. Methanoic acid (formic acid)
B. Ethanoic acid
C. Citric acid
D. Hydrochloric acid
Answer: A
Explanation: Bee venom contains methanoic (formic) acid, HCOOH. Wasp venom is alkaline (treated differently).

87. Acid rain has a pH:
A. Less than 5.6
B. Equal to 7
C. Greater than 7
D. Equal to 5.6
Answer: A
Explanation: Normal rain pH ≈ 5.6 (CO₂ dissolved). Acid rain (due to SO₂, NOx) has pH < 5.6.

88. The colour of phenolphthalein in alkaline solution is:
A. Pink/fuchsia
B. Colourless
C. Yellow
D. Orange
Answer: A
Explanation: Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid/neutral, pink in base (transition pH 8.3–10.0).

89. A buffer solution is BEST described as:
A. A solution that resists changes in pH on addition of small amounts of acid or base
B. A neutral solution at pH 7
C. A concentrated acid solution
D. A highly dilute solution
Answer: A
Explanation: Buffer = weak acid + conjugate base (or weak base + conjugate acid). Effective within ±1 pH unit of pKa.

90. Which salt undergoes no hydrolysis?
A. NaCl (strong acid + strong base)
B. NH₄Cl
C. CH₃COONa
D. Na₂CO₃
Answer: A
Explanation: NaCl is salt of HCl (strong acid) and NaOH (strong base). Neither ion hydrolyzes → pH = 7.

91. Electrolysis of dilute H₂SO₄ produces at the cathode:
A. Hydrogen gas (H₂)
B. Oxygen gas
C. Sulfur dioxide
D. Water
Answer: A
Explanation: At cathode (reduction): 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂. At anode: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻.

92. In the electrolysis of CuSO₄ with copper electrodes, the copper anode:
A. Dissolves and copper deposits at cathode (refining principle)
B. Produces oxygen
C. Remains unchanged
D. Produces SO₂
Answer: A
Explanation: Copper anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻. Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. Net transfer of Cu from anode to cathode — basis of copper purification.

93. The quantity of electricity (charge) is measured in:
A. Coulombs (C = A × s)
B. Volts
C. Amperes
D. Ohms
Answer: A
Explanation: Q = It (amperes × seconds = coulombs). Faraday = 96500 C/mol of electrons.

94. How many grams of copper are deposited when 2 Faradays pass through CuSO₄ solution? (Cu = 64)
A. 64 g
B. 32 g
C. 128 g
D. 16 g
Answer: A
Explanation: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. 2F needed per mol Cu. 2F gives 1 mol Cu = 64 g.

95. The cell notation for a standard zinc-copper galvanic cell is:
A. Zn|Zn²⁺||Cu²⁺|Cu
B. Cu|Cu²⁺||Zn²⁺|Zn
C. Zn²⁺|Zn||Cu|Cu²⁺
D. Cu²⁺|Cu||Zn|Zn²⁺
Answer: A
Explanation: Anode (oxidation) written first, cathode (reduction) last. Zn is oxidized (anode), Cu²⁺ is reduced (cathode). Emf = +1.10 V.

96. The EMF of a galvanic cell is calculated as:
A. E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode
B. E°cell = E°anode - E°cathode
C. E°cell = E°cathode + E°anode
D. E°cell = E°anode × E°cathode
Answer: A
Explanation: E°cell = E°(reduction at cathode) - E°(reduction at anode). Positive EMF = spontaneous reaction.

97. Corrosion of iron (rusting) is an example of:
A. Electrochemical oxidation
B. Physical change
C. Reduction
D. Neutralization
Answer: A
Explanation: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation at anode). O₂ + H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (reduction at cathode). Water acts as electrolyte.

98. Which method is used to prevent rusting by sacrificial protection?
A. Attaching a more reactive metal (Zn, Mg) to iron
B. Painting the surface
C. Electroplating with copper
D. Alloying with nickel
Answer: A
Explanation: Sacrificial anode: Zn is oxidized preferentially, protecting Fe. Used on ships, pipelines, oil rigs.

99. Electrolytic refining of aluminium uses:
A. Cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) as solvent for Al₂O₃
B. Dilute H₂SO₄ as electrolyte
C. Molten NaCl
D. Aqueous AlCl₃
Answer: A
Explanation: Hall-Héroult process: Al₂O₃ dissolved in molten cryolite at 950°C. Electrolysis deposits Al at cathode.

100. In electrolysis of molten NaCl, the product at the anode is:
A. Chlorine gas (Cl₂)
B. Sodium metal
C. Hydrogen gas
D. Oxygen gas
Answer: A
Explanation: Anode (oxidation): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻. Cathode (reduction): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na. Basis of chlor-alkali process (with NaCl solution).

101. The Nernst equation relates cell EMF to:
A. Concentration of reactants and products
B. Temperature only
C. Pressure only
D. Catalyst concentration
Answer: A
Explanation: E = E° - (RT/nF)lnQ. At non-standard concentrations, cell potential differs from standard. Useful for pH meters.

102. In the electroplating of silver, the anode should be:
A. Pure silver
B. Carbon (graphite)
C. Copper
D. Zinc
Answer: A
Explanation: Silver anode dissolves to replenish Ag⁺ ions in solution, maintaining concentration. Object to be plated is cathode.

103. Which of the following metals can be extracted by electrolysis of its aqueous salt solution?
A. Copper
B. Sodium
C. Calcium
D. Aluminium
Answer: A
Explanation: Metals below hydrogen in reactivity series can be deposited from aqueous solution. Na, Ca, Al are too reactive — H₂ would form instead; they need molten electrolysis.

104. The reduction half-reaction at the cathode during electrolysis of dilute NaCl is:
A. 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
B. 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
C. Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na
D. 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻
Answer: A
Explanation: In dilute NaCl (aqueous), H₂O is preferentially reduced at cathode (not Na⁺ — too reactive). Product: H₂ gas + OH⁻.

105. Faraday's second law states that for the same quantity of electricity:
A. Masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses
B. All metals deposit in equal masses
C. Mass is proportional to current
D. Deposition rate is constant
Answer: A
Explanation: m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ (ratio of equivalent masses). Allows comparison of deposits from different electrolytes with same charge.

106. The standard electrode potential of zinc is -0.76 V. This means zinc:
A. Is more easily oxidized than hydrogen
B. Is less reactive than hydrogen
C. Deposits at +0.76 V
D. Cannot act as anode
Answer: A
Explanation: Negative E° means metal is oxidized more easily than H₂ (standard). More negative = more reactive metal.

107. Which of the following electrolytes would give oxygen at the anode?
A. Dilute H₂SO₄
B. Concentrated HCl
C. Molten NaCl
D. CuSO₄ with copper electrodes
Answer: A
Explanation: At inert anode in dilute H₂SO₄: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻. In concentrated HCl or molten NaCl, Cl₂ is produced instead.

108. The electrolysis of brine (concentrated NaCl) produces all of the following EXCEPT:
A. Nitrogen gas
B. Chlorine gas
C. Hydrogen gas
D. Sodium hydroxide
Answer: A
Explanation: Chlor-alkali products: Cl₂ (anode), H₂ (cathode), NaOH (in solution). Nitrogen is not produced.

109. If 9650 C of charge is passed, the moles of electrons transferred are:
A. 0.1 mol
B. 1 mol
C. 0.01 mol
D. 10 mol
Answer: A
Explanation: moles e⁻ = Q/F = 9650/96500 = 0.1 mol.

110. Cathodic protection of iron uses:
A. Zinc or magnesium blocks attached to the iron
B. A layer of paint
C. Electrical insulation
D. Alloying with chromium
Answer: A
Explanation: Sacrificial metals (Zn, Mg) with more negative electrode potential corrode preferentially, protecting iron from oxidation.

111. At equilibrium, the rate of the forward reaction:
A. Equals the rate of the reverse reaction
B. Is greater than the reverse
C. Is less than the reverse
D. Is zero
Answer: A
Explanation: Dynamic equilibrium: both reactions continue but at equal rates so concentrations remain constant.

112. For the reaction PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂, Kc at 250°C is 0.04. If [PCl₅]=0.2, [PCl₃]=0.1, [Cl₂]=0.1 initially, the reaction will:
A. Proceed forward (Q < Kc)
B. Proceed backward
C. Be at equilibrium
D. Stop completely
Answer: A
Explanation: Q = [PCl₃][Cl₂]/[PCl₅] = (0.1)(0.1)/0.2 = 0.05. Q > Kc (0.04) → reaction shifts backward. (This is a corrected explanation — Q > Kc means reaction goes backward; if Q < Kc it goes forward.)
Note: Answer should be B (backward) if Q=0.05 > Kc=0.04. This tests direction prediction.
Corrected Answer: B
Explanation: Q = 0.05 > Kc = 0.04 → reaction proceeds backward (right to left) until Q = Kc.

113. Increasing temperature of an exothermic reaction shifts equilibrium:
A. To the left (favors reverse endothermic direction)
B. To the right
C. No effect
D. Increases Kc
Answer: A
Explanation: Le Chatelier: adding heat to exothermic system → system reduces heat → shifts backward. Kc decreases with increasing T for exothermic reactions.

114. The unit of the rate constant k for a second-order reaction is:
A. L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
B. mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
C. s⁻¹
D. L² mol⁻² s⁻¹
Answer: A
Explanation: Rate = k[A]² → k = Rate/[A]² = (mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹)/(mol L⁻¹)² = L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.

115. The half-life of a first-order reaction is:
A. Independent of initial concentration
B. Proportional to initial concentration
C. Inversely proportional to concentration squared
D. Equal to 1/k
Answer: A
Explanation: t½ = 0.693/k for first-order. Only for second-order: t½ = 1/k[A]₀ (depends on concentration).

116. A catalyst lowers activation energy. This means:
A. More molecules have sufficient energy to react
B. Temperature increases
C. Equilibrium shifts to products
D. Reaction becomes exothermic
Answer: A
Explanation: Maxwell-Boltzmann: fraction of molecules above Ea increases when Ea decreases. Rate constant k = Ae^(-Ea/RT).

117. If reaction rate doubles for every 10°C rise, the rate at 50°C compared to 20°C is:
A. 8 times
B. 4 times
C. 2 times
D. 16 times
Answer: A
Explanation: ΔT = 30°C = 3 intervals of 10°C. Rate multiplier = 2³ = 8.

118. For the reaction 2A + B → C, rate = k[A][B]. This reaction is:
A. Second order overall (first in A, first in B)
B. Third order
C. Second order in A
D. Zero order
Answer: A
Explanation: Order = sum of exponents = 1+1 = 2 (second order). Stoichiometric coefficients ≠ rate law orders unless proven.

119. The Contact process for making H₂SO₄ uses which catalyst?
A. Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)
B. Iron
C. Platinum
D. Nickel
Answer: A
Explanation: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃. V₂O₅ catalyst at 450°C. SO₃ then absorbed in H₂SO₄ to give oleum.

120. Adding an inert gas to an equilibrium at constant volume:
A. Has no effect on equilibrium position
B. Shifts equilibrium to the side with more moles
C. Always shifts equilibrium forward
D. Increases Kp
Answer: A
Explanation: Partial pressures of reactants/products unchanged at constant volume → no shift. (At constant pressure, it would shift.)

121. Kp is related to Kc by:
A. Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn
B. Kp = Kc/RT
C. Kp = Kc × Δn
D. Kp = Kc²
Answer: A
Explanation: Δn = moles gaseous products - moles gaseous reactants. If Δn=0, Kp=Kc.

122. Activation energy (Ea) can be determined from:
A. A graph of ln k vs 1/T (Arrhenius plot)
B. Measuring product concentration
C. Titration
D. Calorimetry directly
Answer: A
Explanation: ln k = ln A - Ea/RT. Plot ln k vs 1/T: slope = -Ea/R. Used to determine activation energy experimentally.

123. In the Ostwald process for nitric acid, ammonia is oxidized to NO. The catalyst used is:
A. Platinum-rhodium gauze
B. V₂O₅
C. Iron
D. Nickel
Answer: A
Explanation: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt/Rh, 900°C). NO then oxidized to NO₂, dissolved in water to give HNO₃.

124. A zero-order reaction has a rate that:
A. Is independent of reactant concentration
B. Doubles when concentration doubles
C. Quadruples when concentration doubles
D. Halves when concentration doubles
Answer: A
Explanation: Rate = k[A]⁰ = k. Rate constant equals rate. Example: enzyme-catalyzed reactions at high substrate concentration.

125. Le Chatelier's principle applies to:
A. Any system in dynamic equilibrium subjected to a change
B. Only gas-phase reactions
C. Only ionic reactions
D. Irreversible reactions only
Answer: A
Explanation: Applies to chemical equilibria, phase equilibria, dissolution equilibria. Any change (T, P, concentration) is counteracted by the system.

126. The solubility product Ksp of AgCl is 1×10⁻¹⁰. Its molar solubility is:
A. 1×10⁻⁵ mol/L
B. 1×10⁻¹⁰ mol/L
C. 1×10⁻² mol/L
D. 1×10⁻⁷ mol/L
Answer: A
Explanation: AgCl ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻. Ksp = s² = 10⁻¹⁰, so s = 10⁻⁵ mol/L.

127. The common ion effect:
A. Decreases the solubility of a sparingly soluble salt
B. Increases solubility
C. Has no effect on solubility
D. Only applies to acids
Answer: A
Explanation: Adding a common ion shifts equilibrium away from dissolution (Le Chatelier). E.g., adding NaCl to AgCl solution decreases AgCl solubility.

128. The order of reaction is determined by:
A. Experimental rate measurements at different concentrations
B. Stoichiometry of the balanced equation
C. The value of Kc
D. The activation energy
Answer: A
Explanation: Order must be found experimentally; cannot be predicted from the equation alone (only for elementary reactions).

129. Temperature affects reaction rate because:
A. Higher T increases both collision frequency and fraction of molecules with energy ≥ Ea
B. Higher T only increases collision frequency
C. Higher T changes the activation energy
D. Higher T increases bond strength
Answer: A
Explanation: Arrhenius: both collision frequency (√T dependence) and Boltzmann factor (exponential) increase. The exponential factor dominates.

130. Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium in which direction for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃?
A. Forward (decreases moles of gas: 4→2)
B. Backward
C. No effect
D. Depends on temperature
Answer: A
Explanation: Δn(gas) = 2-4 = -2. High pressure favors fewer moles of gas → forward. Haber process uses 200 atm.

131. For an endothermic reaction, the forward activation energy (Ea forward) compared to reverse (Ea reverse) is:
A. Greater (Ea forward > Ea reverse)
B. Less
C. Equal
D. Zero
Answer: A
Explanation: ΔH = Ea(forward) - Ea(reverse). For endothermic ΔH > 0, so Ea(f) > Ea(r).

132. A reaction is said to be autocatalytic when:
A. A product of the reaction acts as a catalyst
B. A reactant is its own catalyst
C. No catalyst is required
D. The reaction accelerates due to temperature rise only
Answer: A
Explanation: Example: oxidation of oxalic acid by KMnO₄ where Mn²⁺ product catalyzes the reaction — rate increases as product forms.

133. The equilibrium constant Kc has no units when:
A. Δn = 0 (moles of products = moles of reactants)
B. All species are gases
C. Kc > 1
D. Reaction is fast
Answer: A
Explanation: Kc units depend on Δn. If Δn=0, concentrations cancel → dimensionless. E.g., H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI (Δn=0).

134. Which factor does NOT affect the value of Kc?
A. Adding a catalyst
B. Changing temperature
C. Changing concentrations
D. All of the above affect Kc
Answer: A
Explanation: Only temperature changes Kc. Catalysts and concentration changes affect rate or position of equilibrium but not the equilibrium constant itself.

135. In the decomposition of N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, degree of dissociation increases when:
A. Pressure decreases (or volume increases)
B. Pressure increases
C. Temperature decreases
D. A catalyst is removed
Answer: A
Explanation: Decreasing pressure favors more moles of gas → more NO₂ → greater dissociation. Also increases with increasing temperature (endothermic reaction).

140. Markovnikov's rule states that in addition of HX to an alkene:

142. The process of cracking produces:

D. Oxygen-containing compounds

Explanation: Thermal or catalytic cracking breaks C–C bonds in long-chain alkanes, producing alkenes (valuable for plastics) and smaller alkanes.

143. Benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution rather than addition because:

A. Chain (skeletal) isomerism
B. Positional isomerism
C. Functional group isomerism
D. Optical isomerism
Answer: A
Explanation: E.g., butane (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃) vs isobutane (CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃). Same formula C₄H₁₀ but different connectivity.

158. The reaction of an alcohol with sodium metal produces:
A. Hydrogen gas (alkoxide + H₂)
B. Water
C. Salt + CO₂
D. Aldehyde
Answer: A
Explanation: 2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂. Similar to Na + H₂O but less vigorous. Confirms acidic H on –OH.

159. Which of the following is an aromatic compound?
A. Benzene (C₆H₆)
B. Cyclohexane
C. Hexane
D. Cyclohexene
Answer: A
Explanation: Aromatic: planar, cyclic, (4n+2)π electrons (Hückel). Benzene = 6π electrons (n=1). Cyclohexane is aliphatic saturated.

160. The addition of HBr to propene gives mainly:
A. 2-bromopropane (Markovnikov product)
B. 1-bromopropane
C. Propane
D. Propyne
Answer: A
Explanation: CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃. H adds to CH (more H), Br adds to CH (more substituted), forming 2° carbocation intermediate.

161. Condensation polymers differ from addition polymers in that:
A. Condensation polymers release a small molecule (e.g. H₂O) during formation
B. Condensation polymers use unsaturated monomers
C. Addition polymers need two different monomers
D. Condensation polymers are always synthetic
Answer: A
Explanation: Nylon (polyamide), polyester, proteins — all condensation polymers releasing H₂O or HCl. Addition polymers: polyethylene, PVC, polypropylene.

162. The dehydration of ethanol at 170°C with concentrated H₂SO₄ gives:
A. Ethene (elimination)
B. Diethyl ether
C. Ethanoic acid
D. Ethane
Answer: A
Explanation: High temperature (>170°C) favors elimination → ethene. Lower temperature (140°C) favors substitution → diethyl ether.

163. Which test distinguishes CH₃CHO from CH₃COCH₃?
A. Tollens' reagent (silver mirror)
B. Bromine water
C. Litmus paper
D. Heating
Answer: A
Explanation: Aldehyde (CH₃CHO) gives positive silver mirror; ketone (CH₃COCH₃) does not. Both decolorize Br₂ water under certain conditions.

164. The empirical formula of benzene is:
A. CH
B. C₂H₂
C. C₆H₆
D. C₃H₃
Answer: A
Explanation: Simplest ratio: C₆H₆ → C:H = 1:1 → CH. Molecular formula C₆H₆ = 6 × (CH).

165. The type of polymerization that forms nylon is:
A. Condensation polymerization
B. Addition polymerization
C. Free radical polymerization
D. Ionic polymerization
Answer: A
Explanation: Nylon-6,6: diamine + dicarboxylic acid → polyamide + H₂O. Condensation = loss of small molecule with each bond.

166. Petroleum refining separates fractions based on:
A. Differences in boiling points (fractional distillation)
B. Density
C. Solubility in water
D. Molecular mass directly
Answer: A
Explanation: Crude oil heated; vapors rise and condense at different heights in fractionating column. Smaller molecules have lower bp and rise higher.

167. Which compound will undergo addition reactions most readily?
A. Ethyne (triple bond, most unsaturated)
B. Ethane
C. Propane
D. Benzene
Answer: A
Explanation: Triple bond (ethyne) > double bond (ethene) in reactivity toward addition. Benzene resists addition to maintain aromaticity.

168. The process by which proteins are broken down into amino acids is called:
A. Hydrolysis
B. Polymerization
C. Esterification
D. Saponification
Answer: A
Explanation: Peptide bonds (amide bonds) hydrolyzed by acid/base/enzymes → constituent amino acids.

169. Optical isomers (enantiomers) are possible when a molecule has:
A. A chiral carbon (asymmetric carbon — 4 different groups)
B. A double bond
C. A triple bond
D. A ring structure
Answer: A
Explanation: Chiral C has no plane of symmetry → non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers) → rotate plane-polarized light.

170. The compound CH₃–O–CH₃ is:
A. Dimethyl ether (an ether)
B. Methanol
C. Dimethyl ketone
D. Methanoic acid
Answer: A
Explanation: R–O–R' structure = ether. Functional group isomers with alcohols (C₂H₆O: CH₃OCH₃ and CH₃CH₂OH).

171. Which reaction converts alkyl halides to alcohols?
A. Nucleophilic substitution with OH⁻ (hydrolysis)
B. Electrophilic addition
C. Elimination
D. Oxidation
Answer: A
Explanation: RX + NaOH(aq) → ROH + NaX. OH⁻ is nucleophile replacing halide. Competing elimination gives alkene.

172. The polymerization of amino acids forms:
A. Proteins (via peptide bonds –CO–NH–)
B. Carbohydrates
C. Lipids
D. Nucleic acids
Answer: A
Explanation: –COOH of one amino acid + –NH₂ of next → –CO–NH– (peptide bond) + H₂O. Sequence → primary structure → protein folding.

173. Addition polymerization requires monomers that contain:
A. Double or triple bonds (π bonds)
B. Two different functional groups
C. A ring structure
D. Halogen atoms
Answer: A
Explanation: π bonds break to link monomers. E.g., ethene → polyethylene, styrene → polystyrene, vinyl chloride → PVC.

174. The ester formed from methanol and ethanoic acid is:
A. Methyl ethanoate (CH₃COOCH₃)
B. Ethyl methanoate
C. Methyl methanoate
D. Ethyl ethanoate
Answer: A
Explanation: Alcohol portion names the alkyl group (methyl from methanol); acid names the acyl group (ethanoate from ethanoic acid) → methyl ethanoate.

175. Which of the following exhibits all three — chain, position, and functional group isomerism — given molecular formula C₃H₆O?
A. Propanal, propan-2-one, and propenol
B. Propane and propene
C. Glycerol
D. None of the above
Answer: A
Explanation: C₃H₆O gives: propanal (aldehyde), propan-2-one (ketone, functional group isomer), and allyl alcohol CH₂=CHCH₂OH (positional/functional). Rich isomerism.

SECTION H: STATES OF MATTER & GAS LAWS (Q176–190)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

176. Boyle's law states that at constant temperature:
A. PV = constant (pressure and volume are inversely proportional)
B. P/T = constant
C. V/T = constant
D. PV/T = constant
Answer: A
Explanation: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ at constant T and n. Gas molecules hit walls less often as volume increases → lower pressure.

177. Charles' law states that at constant pressure:
A. V/T = constant (volume proportional to absolute temperature)
B. PV = constant
C. P/T = constant
D. VT = constant
Answer: A
Explanation: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂. Temperature in Kelvin. Extrapolation to V=0 gives absolute zero (0 K = -273°C).

178. The ideal gas equation is:
A. PV = nRT
B. PV = RT
C. P = nRT/V²
D. PV = nR/T
Answer: A
Explanation: Combines Boyle's, Charles', and Avogadro's laws. R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (or 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹).

179. A gas at 27°C and 1 atm is heated to 127°C at constant volume. The new pressure is:
A. 4/3 atm (≈ 1.33 atm)
B. 2 atm
C. 0.75 atm
D. 1 atm
Answer: A
Explanation: Gay-Lussac's law: P/T = const. T₁=300K, T₂=400K. P₂ = P₁×(T₂/T₁) = 1×(400/300) = 4/3 atm.

180. Dalton's law of partial pressures states:
A. Total pressure = sum of partial pressures of all gases
B. Gases exert equal pressure
C. Pressure is independent of gas type
D. At STP all gases have equal partial pressure
Answer: A
Explanation: P_total = P_A + P_B + P_C + ... Each gas behaves independently in a mixture.

181. According to Graham's law, the rate of diffusion of a gas is proportional to:
A. 1/√(molar mass)
B. √(molar mass)
C. molar mass
D. 1/molar mass
Answer: A
Explanation: r ∝ 1/√M. Lighter gases diffuse faster. r₁/r₂ = √(M₂/M₁).

182. The kinetic theory of gases assumes:
A. Gas molecules have negligible volume and no intermolecular forces
B. Gas molecules attract each other
C. Gas molecules have fixed positions
D. Temperature does not affect speed
Answer: A
Explanation: Ideal gas assumptions: point particles, elastic collisions, no attractive/repulsive forces. Real gases deviate at high P and low T.

183. Which gas would deviate MOST from ideal behavior?
A. SO₂ (polar, large)
B. He (small, noble)
C. H₂ (small, nonpolar)
D. N₂ (small, nonpolar)
Answer: A
Explanation: Deviation ∝ intermolecular forces (related to polarity and size). SO₂ is polar with significant van der Waals interactions.

184. The root mean square speed of gas molecules is proportional to:
A. √(T/M)
B. T/M
C. √M
D. M/T
Answer: A
Explanation: u_rms = √(3RT/M). Speed increases with temperature, decreases with molar mass.

185. At the same temperature, which gas has the highest average kinetic energy?
A. All gases have the same average KE (depends only on T)
B. H₂ (lightest)
C. O₂ (heavier)
D. CO₂ (heaviest)
Answer: A
Explanation: Average KE = (3/2)RT per mole — depends only on temperature, not molar mass. Lighter molecules move faster but have same average energy.

186. A gas collected over water has a total pressure of 760 mmHg. If the vapor pressure of water is 20 mmHg, the partial pressure of the gas is:
A. 740 mmHg
B. 780 mmHg
C. 760 mmHg
D. 20 mmHg
Answer: A
Explanation: P_gas = P_total - P_water = 760 - 20 = 740 mmHg (Dalton's law).

187. The van der Waals constant 'a' corrects for:
A. Intermolecular attractions (reduces actual pressure)
B. Molecular volume
C. Temperature effect
D. Pressure of real gas
Answer: A
Explanation: (P + a/V²)(V - b) = RT. 'a' increases effective pressure (molecules don't hit walls as hard due to attractions). 'b' corrects for actual volume.

188. Absolute zero is the temperature at which:
A. Gas molecules have zero kinetic energy theoretically
B. Water freezes
C. All gases liquefy
D. Volume of gas equals zero actually
Answer: A
Explanation: 0 K = -273.15°C. Theoretical limit. Third law of thermodynamics: entropy → constant as T → 0 K. Not actually reachable.

189. Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes was instrumental in:
A. Determining molecular formulas of gaseous compounds
B. Calculating atomic masses
C. Developing the mole concept
D. Understanding liquid behavior
Answer: A
Explanation: E.g., 1 vol N₂ + 3 vol H₂ → 2 vol NH₃ directly implies N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ (Avogadro's hypothesis).

190. An ideal gas is compressed from 10 L to 5 L at constant temperature. The pressure:
A. Doubles
B. Halves
C. Stays the same
D. Quadruples
Answer: A
Explanation: Boyle's law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. V halved → P doubled.

SECTION I: METALS, EXTRACTION & ENVIRONMENTAL CHEMISTRY (Q191–200)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

191. The thermite reaction uses aluminium to reduce:
A. Iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃) → iron metal (Al more reactive)
B. Copper oxide
C. Zinc oxide
D. Lead oxide
Answer: A
Explanation: 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe. Highly exothermic. Used in welding railway tracks. Demonstrates relative reactivity.

192. The main ore of aluminium is:
A. Bauxite (Al₂O₃·2H₂O)
B. Haematite
C. Galena
D. Malachite
Answer: A
Explanation: Purified to Al₂O₃ (alumina) then electrolyzed in Hall-Héroult process. Haematite = iron ore, Galena = lead, Malachite = copper.

193. Which gas is primarily responsible for the greenhouse effect?
A. CO₂ (along with CH₄, H₂O vapour)
B. N₂
C. O₂
D. Ar
Answer: A
Explanation: CO₂ absorbs infrared radiation re-emitted by Earth's surface. CH₄ is more potent per molecule but less abundant.

194. The ozone layer is destroyed mainly by:
A. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) releasing Cl• radicals
B. Carbon dioxide
C. Sulfur dioxide
D. Nitrogen gas
Answer: A
Explanation: Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂; ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂. Each Cl atom destroys ~100,000 ozone molecules catalytically.

195. Acid rain is primarily caused by:
A. SO₂ and NOx reacting with water in the atmosphere
B. CO₂ only
C. HCl emissions
D. Ozone depletion
Answer: A
Explanation: SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄; NOx → HNO₃. Sources: combustion of sulfur-containing fuels, vehicle emissions.

196. Galvanizing protects iron by coating it with:
A. Zinc (more reactive, provides sacrificial protection)
B. Tin
C. Copper
D. Lead
Answer: A
Explanation: Zinc coating: (1) physical barrier; (2) if scratched, Zn (more reactive) oxidizes preferentially, protecting Fe.

197. The blast furnace produces iron using:
A. Coke (carbon) as reducing agent and limestone as flux
B. Aluminium as reductant
C. Electrolysis of iron ore
D. Direct reduction with H₂ only
Answer: A
Explanation: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (CO from coke combustion). Limestone (CaCO₃) → CaO + CO₂; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag removes silica impurities).

198. The major pollutant formed when petrol engines run on rich fuel (excess fuel) is:
A. Carbon monoxide (CO)
B. SO₂
C. NO₂
D. Ozone
Answer: A
Explanation: Incomplete combustion (insufficient O₂) gives CO instead of CO₂. CO is toxic — binds hemoglobin (>CO₂ affinity ×250).

199. Hard water contains dissolved:
A. Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions (from limestone, gypsum)
B. Na⁺ and K⁺ ions
C. Cl⁻ ions
D. Fe³⁺ ions
Answer: A
Explanation: Temporary hardness (HCO₃⁻): removed by boiling. Permanent hardness (SO₄²⁻, Cl⁻): removed by ion exchange or Na₂CO₃.

200. The process of zone refining is used to obtain:
A. Ultra-pure semiconductors (e.g. silicon, germanium)
B. Pure salt
C. Pure copper by electrolysis
D. Pure aluminium
Answer: A
Explanation: A narrow molten zone is passed along a rod; impurities concentrate in the melt and move to one end. Produces semiconductor-grade purity (>99.9999%).
